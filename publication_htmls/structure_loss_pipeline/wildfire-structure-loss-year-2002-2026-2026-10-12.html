Wildfire structure loss

Wildfire structure loss by year, 2002–2026 · 2026-10-12

Buildings destroyed per year in the conterminous US, from two federal incident-report series, the USGS Carlson hand-mapped record and our satellite model. 2026* is a partial year, through October.

By Carlo Broderick, NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara · 2026-10-12 14:59 PDT

Losses swing hugely from year to year, and the big years (2017, 2018, 2020, 2025) are each made by a few fires. The number of fires that destroy any building has not risen.

How to read this page

Each line is one source. They count differently, so compare the shape of each line, not their heights. Hover any point for that year's details; switch to Technical for definitions.

1. Buildings destroyed each year

Buildings destroyed per year, by source

The incident reports and Carlson rise and fall together. Carlson is lower because it maps only fires that destroyed 10 or more buildings; our model is lower because it finds about two-thirds of losses.Incident-report counts are reported structures destroyed (all structure types); Carlson counts hand-mapped destroyed buildings in fires with ≥10 destroyed; the model counts first-call destroyed buildings in the Direct zone (end-to-end recall ≈0.61 on 40 unseen fires).

3. The number of fires with losses is not rising

Fires that destroyed at least one building, per year

ICS-209-PLUS: 285 a year in 2002–11, 254 in 2012–20. Situation reports: 127 a year in 2007–16, 130 in 2017–25. The rise in losses comes from bigger fires, not more of them.Annual means of incidents with STR_DESTROYED ≥ 1, by start year, CONUS. The two series differ in level because ICS-209-PLUS includes every incident with a report and IMSR only those in the national situation summaries.

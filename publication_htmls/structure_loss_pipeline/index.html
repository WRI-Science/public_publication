Structure Loss Pipeline

A reproducible pipeline that finds the structures standing before a wildfire and predicts which of them were destroyed, from public data only — so the USGS Carlson structure-loss record (2002–2022) can be continued to the present and rolled forward as new fires happen.

what this is

The project in four paragraphs

The USGS Carlson building-loss dataset is a per-building census of what wildfires destroyed in the conterminous United States between 2002 and 2022 — 361 fires, 1,770,939 rows, hand digitised. It is the reference layer for anyone modelling wildfire exposure, and it stops at 2022. This project continues it with a pipeline that runs on public data only: NAIP aerial imagery and the USA Structures and Microsoft footprint databases to enumerate the buildings that stood before a fire, then before-and-after Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 at each building to decide which of them burned.

The two halves are judged separately, against different truth sets, because they are different mistakes. The inventory is scored against Carlson's census — which enumerates every major building inside a fire's footprint, so a missing building is measurable — and the loss call against CAL FIRE inspections of fires that happened after Carlson ends, which is the only truly out-of-sample evidence that exists. The honest summary of the first half is that the public footprint databases are the product and our detector is slightly better than them: it recovers 343 buildings across fifteen fires, 3.5 % of what those databases missed. The honest summary of the second is that the loss call is good — mean per-fire F1 0.799 against the inspection record, κ 0.883 against Carlson's own hand classification — and that the evidence behind it is eight California fires, two of which hold 84 % of the structures.

The output is one layer on one schema, one row per structure per fire, with Carlson's rows and ours side by side and a field only one era can fill left null on the other rather than back-filled. Every row carries a continuous destroyed probability, the threshold behind the call, the frame and exposure class the structure sat in, and per-row provenance saying which layer found it and which model scored it. Release v0.3.0 is 399,682 rows over 20 fires, MIT for the code and CC BY 4.0 for the data.

Two rules shape everything here. No attribute of the fire itself ever reaches the model — not size, perimeter, dNBR, region, state or year — so a loss call is never a restatement of the fire's own attributes; perimeters decide where to look and never what to predict. And every promotion bar is written down before the number that would clear it is measured, which is why this site publishes results that point away from what shipped: a component of the production model that no longer clears its own promotion rule, and a cut rule that now clears a bar that once rejected it.

status

Where the project is, 12 September 2026

Published here is what is committed and on disk: release v0.3.0 (cut 2026-09-11) and the v4 corrected-sampler metric tables. The production recipe behind every number on this site is the detector seg_all_v4 on each fire's own public-perimeter frame, the damage bundle damage_sentinel2s1_v4, and a fixed cut of 0.87.

Three pieces of work were in flight while these pages were written, and none of their results appear on this site:

  • in flight Roadmap 11 — v0.4.0. On 2026-09-11 the owner decided the licence, adopted a per-fire operating rule in place of the fixed cut, and dropped Sentinel-1 radar from production. The optical-only bundle was written to disk on 2026-09-12; its folds, the re-score of every fire and the release that would publish them do not exist yet. The evidence behind both decisions is published, in the deep dive and on the outside-California page.
  • in flight Roadmap 12 — the 2016–2022 retrospective. Part 1 (which fires can carry the era comparison, and the damage half on 23 fires instead of 10) landed 2026-09-12 and is reported, marked as such. Part 2, the structure count on seventeen rebuilt inventories, is still running.
  • in flight Roadmap 13 — augmented Carlson rasters. Nothing from it is reported.

These pages are refreshed by re-reading the same files. Every number on this site names the file it came from, in a source line under the table that uses it. Where a published claim is known to have moved, the older number is kept beside the newer one rather than deleted.

provenance

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this site is transcribed from a file the pipeline wrote — a metrics.json, a summary.json, a per-fire CSV or a release manifest — and the source line under each table names it. Nothing on these pages is recomputed, and nothing is recalled from memory. The internal record behind them is the project's dated change log and an experiment ledger with one row for every idea, experiment, data source and decision, including the ones that failed; both live in the pipeline repository alongside the scripts that produced every output cited here.

Structure inventory · coverage vs Carlson

What is this map?

What you are looking at

This is the pipeline's step-1 product for the Marshall Fire (CO, 2021): the structures that stood before the fire, assembled from public data only. Every USA Structures footprint dated before ignition, plus every building the U-Net found on 2021 NAIP aerial imagery that no USA Structures point explained — 14,103 plus 214 = 14,786 points.

The question this map answers is coverage: does that union contain the buildings Carlson actually recorded? The rings are Carlson's points — red where the building was destroyed (B), green where it survived (U). The stats chip gives the coverage with USA Structures alone and with the union, so the U-Net's contribution is the difference between them.

Nothing on this map is a damage prediction. This unit is a training fire, so a damage call here would be scored against labels the model has already seen.

Plain-language glossary

  • Carlson — independent building points from Carlson et al. 2025 (USGS data release doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Every point is a building assessed after a U.S. wildfire, 2002–2022. These are evaluation data: they were never used to train the building-detection model.
  • B / U — Carlson's damage codes. B = destroyed / burned; U = surviving / unburned.
  • Chip — a small square aerial photo (512×512 pixels, about 0.6 m per pixel) cut from USDA NAIP imagery. The detection model is trained and run on these tiles.
  • Found / missed — a Carlson point is found when a predicted structure point falls within 20 m of it, and missed otherwise. 20 m is the pipeline's matching radius (validate.match_radius_m).
  • In label — the Carlson point sits inside a USA Structures polygon, i.e. inside a building the model was actually taught to find. Points outside every label are buildings the training labels never showed the model.
  • Precision — of the buildings the model predicted, the share that match a Carlson point. Recall — of Carlson's buildings, the share the model found. F1 — their harmonic mean, one number that punishes being bad at either.
  • Out-of-fold / leave-one-fire-out — every fire is scored by a model trained on the other fires only, so a number for a fire never comes from a model that saw that fire.
  • TP / FP / FN / TN — on the damage maps: predicted destroyed and was (TP), predicted destroyed but survived (FP), predicted surviving but was destroyed (FN), predicted surviving and survived (TN).

Inventory — USA Structures

What you see: one dot per building the inventory got from USA Structures, 14,103 of them, thin white ring. The fill is the predicted probability the building was destroyed: green near 0, yellow in the middle, red near 1, grey where nothing could be scored. Hover for the probability, the occupancy class, the footprint area, and how many clear satellite scenes existed before and after the fire — a point scored from three scenes deserves less trust than one scored from twelve.

Source: FEMA / ORNL USA Structures, filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before ignition. Post-fire footprint products are never used: destroyed buildings are missing from them by construction.

Inventory — U-Net only

What you see: the 214 buildings the U-Net found on NAIP that no USA Structures point explained, drawn with a loud cyan ring. These are the pipeline's own additions and the reason step 1 exists at all — turn the USA Structures layer off to see them alone.

How to read them: a cyan ring sitting on a rooftop with no white ring nearby is a real gain. A cyan ring on a driveway, a pool, or a shipping container is a false positive. Both are here; the map does not filter them.

Source: the U-Net checkpoint run over whole NAIP items, vectorised, then thinned to points more than 20 m from any USA Structures centroid.

Carlson (the answer sheet)

What you see: hollow rings — Carlson building points for this fire. Red = destroyed (B); green = surviving (U). Evaluation only: Carlson was never a training label for the detection model.

Source: raw/carlson/{Fire_ID}.gpkg, the per-fire extract of Carlson et al. 2025.

Fire-wide grid

What you see: the whole unit summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the share of inventory points in that cell that the U-Net added because no USA Structures footprint explained them — pale yellow low, dark red high, grey where a cell holds fewer than three points. Every inventory point is in exactly one cell, so this layer is the honest fire-wide view that the detail windows cannot give you.

How much of the fire you are seeing

This fire has 14,786 points. Drawing them all would make a file no browser opens, so the map shows two things at two scales:

  • Detail windows — 7 squares of 800 m: the 6 with the most points plus the square with the most model failures outside them. Everything inside them is drawn point by point (3,310 points in total). Use the Jump buttons to move between them.
  • Fire-wide grid — every point in the fire summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the rate in that cell. No point is left out of the grid; the cell size grows automatically if a fire would need more than 6,000 cells.

The numbers on this map

Coverage is the share of Carlson points with an inventory point within 20 m. The gap between the USA-Structures-only row and the union row is exactly what running the U-Net on NAIP bought. Both were computed by scripts/04_build_structure_inventory.py and are read here from summary.json.

Where the data come from

  • Carlson — Carlson et al. 2025, USGS national structure-loss points 2002–2022 (doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Evaluation only.
  • USA Structures — Oak Ridge National Laboratory / FEMA building outlines (CC BY 4.0), filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before the fire's ignition. The stage-1 training labels.
  • NAIP — USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, 0.6 m 4-band, via the Microsoft Planetary Computer. What the detection model reads.
  • Sentinel-2 L2A / Landsat Collection 2 L2 — before / after reflectance at each point, via the Planetary Computer. What the damage model reads.
  • CAL FIRE DINS — damage inspection records, the out-of-sample check for fires after Carlson ends.
  • Basemap — Esri World Imagery / Street / Topographic tiles (needs a network connection; nothing else on this page does).

The map draws in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326). All distances, cell sizes and matching were computed in projected metres (the Carlson USA Contiguous Albers Equal Area frame) before reprojection.

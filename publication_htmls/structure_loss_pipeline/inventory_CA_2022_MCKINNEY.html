What is this map?

What you are looking at

This is the pipeline's product, run end to end on the Mckinney Fire (CA, 2022) — a fire the damage bundle was fitted on, so it is scored here by its own leave-one-fire-out fold (prob_source=lofo) rather than by the shipped model.

Two independent steps produced it. First, the pre-fire inventory: every USA Structures footprint standing before 2022-07-29, plus every building the U-Net found on 2020 NAIP aerial imagery that no USA Structures point explained — 314 plus 15 = 407 points. Second, the damage call: before / after sentinel2 reflectance at each of those points (2022-07-13 → 2022-07-13 before, 2022-09-09 → 2022-09-09 after) fed to the damage model, which calls 168 of them destroyed at a probability cut of 0.85.

The rings are the answer sheet: Carlson. Red ring = the record says the building was destroyed; green ring = it was not; grey ring = the record could not say. Counted here: 212 destroyed, 293 not destroyed.

⚠️ This answer sheet is not a census. It covers what somebody chose to inspect, so an inventory point with no ring around it is far more likely to be a building nobody walked past than a false detection. Read recall off this map — how many recorded structures the inventory found — and not precision.

Plain-language glossary

  • Carlson — independent building points from Carlson et al. 2025 (USGS data release doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Every point is a building assessed after a U.S. wildfire, 2002–2022. These are evaluation data: they were never used to train the building-detection model.
  • B / U — Carlson's damage codes. B = destroyed / burned; U = surviving / unburned.
  • Chip — a small square aerial photo (512×512 pixels, about 0.6 m per pixel) cut from USDA NAIP imagery. The detection model is trained and run on these tiles.
  • Found / missed — a Carlson point is found when a predicted structure point falls within 20 m of it, and missed otherwise. 20 m is the pipeline's matching radius (validate.match_radius_m).
  • In label — the Carlson point sits inside a USA Structures polygon, i.e. inside a building the model was actually taught to find. Points outside every label are buildings the training labels never showed the model.
  • Precision — of the buildings the model predicted, the share that match a Carlson point. Recall — of Carlson's buildings, the share the model found. F1 — their harmonic mean, one number that punishes being bad at either.
  • Out-of-fold / leave-one-fire-out — every fire is scored by a model trained on the other fires only, so a number for a fire never comes from a model that saw that fire.
  • TP / FP / FN / TN — on the damage maps: predicted destroyed and was (TP), predicted destroyed but survived (FP), predicted surviving but was destroyed (FN), predicted surviving and survived (TN).

Inventory — USA Structures

What you see: one dot per building the inventory got from USA Structures, 314 of them, thin white ring. The fill is the predicted probability the building was destroyed: green near 0, yellow in the middle, red near 1, grey where nothing could be scored. Hover for the probability, the occupancy class, the footprint area, and how many clear satellite scenes existed before and after the fire — a point scored from three scenes deserves less trust than one scored from twelve.

Source: FEMA / ORNL USA Structures, filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before ignition. Post-fire footprint products are never used: destroyed buildings are missing from them by construction.

Inventory — U-Net only

What you see: the 15 buildings the U-Net found on NAIP that no USA Structures point explained, drawn with a loud cyan ring. These are the pipeline's own additions and the reason step 1 exists at all — turn the USA Structures layer off to see them alone.

How to read them: a cyan ring sitting on a rooftop with no white ring nearby is a real gain. A cyan ring on a driveway, a pool, or a shipping container is a false positive. Both are here; the map does not filter them.

Source: the U-Net checkpoint run over whole NAIP items, vectorised, then thinned to points more than 20 m from any USA Structures centroid.

Carlson (the answer sheet)

What you see: hollow rings — one record from Carlson. Red = destroyed; green = not destroyed; grey = the record could not say.

Source: the assessment cached by slp.data.damage_external / the fire's own Carlson points. Evaluation only — neither was ever a training label for the detector, and this project ships metrics derived from these records, not the records themselves.

Fire-wide grid

What you see: the whole unit summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the share of inventory points in that cell the model calls destroyed at a probability cut of 0.85 — pale yellow low, dark red high, grey where a cell holds fewer than three points. Every inventory point is in exactly one cell, so this layer is the honest fire-wide view that the detail windows cannot give you.

Damage call — refined (a second model, ~a year later)

What you see: the product's second damage call, drawn as a dashed violet ring on the same 407 of 407 points (100.0%) that carry one. The fill is the same green→yellow→red probability ramp, so the ring tells you which model and the fill tells you how sure. The layer is off by default: the primary call is what this product ships.

⚠️ These are two calls, not one call and its correction. The primary call (damage_sentinel2_v5 at 0.85) is made at ignition +74 days. The refined call (damage_sentinel2_refined_v2 at 0.84) needs the post-fire AlphaEarth annual raster, which is published roughly 12–15 months after the fire burns — so it does not exist when a fire is current, and it is better where it does exist. The two cuts are cuts on two different models' score axes and are not comparable with each other.

In the released series these are the damage_class and damage_class_refined columns. Classify with the primary one, and say which you used; never build a single total out of both.

On this fire the refined model called 162 structures destroyed against the primary call's 168.

How much of the fire you are seeing

This fire has 407 points. Drawing them all would make a file no browser opens, so the map shows two things at two scales:

  • Detail windows — 7 squares of 800 m: the 6 with the most points plus the square with the most model failures outside them. Everything inside them is drawn point by point (115 points in total). Use the Jump buttons to move between them.
  • Fire-wide grid — every point in the fire summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the rate in that cell. No point is left out of the grid; the cell size grows automatically if a fire would need more than 6,000 cells.

Where the data come from

  • Carlson — Carlson et al. 2025, USGS national structure-loss points 2002–2022 (doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Evaluation only.
  • USA Structures — Oak Ridge National Laboratory / FEMA building outlines (CC BY 4.0), filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before the fire's ignition. The stage-1 training labels.
  • NAIP — USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, 0.6 m 4-band, via the Microsoft Planetary Computer. What the detection model reads.
  • Sentinel-2 L2A / Landsat Collection 2 L2 — before / after reflectance at each point, via the Planetary Computer. What the damage model reads.
  • CAL FIRE DINS — damage inspection records, the out-of-sample check for fires after Carlson ends.
  • Basemap — Esri World Imagery / Street / Topographic tiles (needs a network connection; nothing else on this page does).

The map draws in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326). All distances, cell sizes and matching were computed in projected metres (the Carlson USA Contiguous Albers Equal Area frame) before reprojection.

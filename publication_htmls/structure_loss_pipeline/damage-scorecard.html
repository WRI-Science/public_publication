Structure Loss Pipeline · damage scorecard

Did this structure burn?

The owner asked how good we are at that call, and for a confusion matrix against each truth source, "because they're different to some extent." They are more different than that. On the same ground, over sixteen fires, CAL FIRE DINS records between 0.82 and 1.84 destroyed structures for every one USGS Carlson recorded. So there is no single number here — there are three scorecards, each against its own truth, each with the vocabulary written down first.

Everything on this page is the shipped bundle, 13 September 2026

damage_sentinel2_v5 at the fixed cut 0.85 — the recipe v0.4.1 ships — scored on the product's own destroyed_pred column, never on a cut re-derived here. Every per-fire number reproduces outputs/forward_damage/per_fire.csv exactly on all fifteen DINS fires, and every external matrix reproduces outputs/ics209_check/outside_ca/*/metrics.json exactly. This page measures. No default, threshold, bundle, column or config value moved.

01 · the question behind the question

What "85 %" actually is

Four true numbers live in that neighbourhood and they answer four different questions. The one the owner is probably remembering is an F1, and it is a little low: the pooled F1 against DINS is 0.924. The number that actually matters for a loss dataset is recall of destroyed — of the buildings that burned, how many do we find — and that is 0.914.

The four numbers near 85 %, and what each one answers.
The numberValueWhat it answers
pooled accuracy vs DINS0.930“what share of calls are right?” — ⚠️ inflated by the base rate
pooled F1 of destroyed vs DINS0.924the destroyed call, balancing missing against over-calling
pooled F1 of destroyed vs Carlson's own points0.892the same question with the inventory taken out
mean-fire F1 vs DINS, all 15 fires0.783“what should I expect on the next fire?”
pooled recall of destroyed vs DINS0.914“of the buildings that burned, how many do we find?”

Source: outputs/scorecard/dins_pooled.json, carlson_points_pooled.json; both written 2026-09-13 by scripts/26_damage_scorecard.py from the shipped per-fire products.

⚠️ Accuracy is the one number to distrust. It is 0.930 against DINS at a base rate of 0.467 — but against Carlson's own points accuracy is 0.981 at a base rate of 0.090, and a model that called everything surviving would score 0.910 there. Accuracy rises as the fire gets smaller, which is the opposite of informative. Every accuracy on this page is printed beside its base rate.

“Mean-fire” is two numbers and both are honest. Averaging per-fire F1 over all fifteen DINS fires gives 0.783. Averaging over the nine fires with at least 100 scored pairs and at least 10 destroyed structures — the bar slp/eval/uncertainty.py and slp/eval/generalization.py both use — gives 0.891 [0.837, 0.928]. The six fires below the bar hold 1 to 15 inspected destroyed structures each; Line 2024's F1 of 0.400 is one true positive and three false ones.

02 · before any matrix

The two truths are not the same truth

They do not disagree about buildings. They count different buildings, and they speak different alphabets.

Source: docs/combined-series-spec.md §2 (the damage vocabulary) and §B (the counting-unit difference); outputs/combined_retrospective/counts_on_the_same_ground.csv.

⚠️ The counting-unit gap is up to 1.8×, and it has nothing to do with this model. DINS destroyed ÷ Carlson destroyed runs 0.82 to 1.84 over sixteen fires, median 1.24, above 1 on 12 of 16. Zogg 2020 is 1.84; Ranch 2018 is 0.82. That is why this page never pools the two truths and never converts a score against one into a claim about the other.

And the binary is a choice we made. params.dins.damage_recode: Destroyed (>50 %) → B; No Damage / Affected / Minor → U; Major (25–50 %) and Inaccessible → X, counted then dropped, because “half burned” is neither destroyed nor intact and “inaccessible” was never actually inspected. Part 03 shows exactly where the dropped and the recoded classes land.

The three honest sentences

Against CAL FIRE DINS — of the structures DINS inspected and we found, we correctly identify 91.3 % of the ones that burned, and 6.5 % of our destroyed calls are on structures DINS did not call destroyed.

Against Carlson's own hand-digitised points — we correctly identify 88.7 % of the buildings Carlson called destroyed, at 89.6 % precision. This is the damage model with the detector taken out of the picture.

Against Carlson end to end — F1 0.862 on the buildings we find, and we find 83.7 % of Carlson's buildings. Compounded, that is the 0.764 destroyed-count ratio in spec §C.

08 · the hand-off

What the 2,016 DINS errors actually are

1,160 false negatives + 856 false positives, bucketed by a cause that can be measured from a column

Two rules make the table readable. The buckets overlap — a small outbuilding on bare ground scoring 0.8 is in three of them — so they are reported independently here and assigned exclusively, first match in a fixed priority order, for the waterfall. And lift is the column to read, not n: a bucket's lift is its own error rate divided by the error rate over all scored pairs (0.0702). Lift 1.0 means the bucket is big, not guilty.

⚠️ Bucket (a) was defined wrongly once, and the correction is the finding. Defined as the lowest decile of post_n_obs it swallowed 55 % of all pairs at lift 1.1 — because Eaton and Palisades are 84 % of the pairs and both sit at 5–6 observations — and it looked like the largest cause of error when it was merely the largest bucket. Redefined as an absolute rule (≤ 3 clear looks) it holds 37 pairs of 28,715. Cloud and smoke are not the forward story.

Bucket (h) can only ever be a bound. Assigning it needs a second independent assessment of the same building, and no 2023+ fire has one. Marshall CO 2021 does: Carlson against Boulder County's field list disagrees on 16 of 1,032 pairs (1.55 %, κ 0.934); Carlson against FEMA MAC on 24 of 2,666 (0.90 %, κ 0.982). Applied to 28,715 DINS pairs that is 258–445 pairs, i.e. 12.8 % to 22.1 % of our 2,016 errors could be the truth being wrong rather than us. ⚠️ Marshall is the easiest case there is — grassland, suburban, total loss, no canopy — so read it as a floor, never an estimate.

The three largest buckets, and what they imply

  1. (i) the cut band — 30 % of DINS errors at lift 5.8, 32 % of Carlson errors at lift 19–20. A third of every error this model makes is on a structure it scored between 0.70 and 0.90. ⚠️ And re-cutting pooled will not fix it: the pooled own-cut ceiling is 0.85 on DINS and 0.84 on Carlson. The recoverable gap is per fire, and road-14 already showed that no label-free per-fire rule clears the bar. So this bucket says the score needs to separate better in 0.7–0.9, not be cut differently — which is where destroyed_prob's over-confidence lives (val-destroyed-prob-overconfident: below observed frequency in 29 of 30 bins).
  2. (b) bare ground — 32 % of DINS errors, and 443 of the 1,160 false negatives. The one cell radar was shown to help (dmg-s1-helps-bare-ground: Q1 recall 0.827 → 0.881, the largest gain of the four quartiles) — a component road-11 then dropped from production on a bar it no longer cleared. That decision is worth revisiting with this table in hand.
  3. (c) small structures — 25 % of errors at lift 1.40 — and (d) the partial-damage classes — 46 % of all false positives. Two faces of one thing: what the 10 m pixel is actually looking at. An outbuilding does not fill a Sentinel-2 pixel, and an Affected building inside a burned block is a pixel that burned around a house that stood. (d) is not fixable by a better model at 10 m — it is fixable by admitting the score is a severity index and shipping three classes rather than two.

The false negatives are not near-misses. Mean score 0.458 against a cut of 0.85 — the model is not hesitating, it is confident and wrong. And 11 of the 30 false positives are structures DINS recorded as damaged, which is bucket (d) wearing a different hat.

Three leads, all testable and none tested here. (1) Four FN tiles where the DINS point is demonstrably not on the building we scored — which bucket (f)'s distance test does not catch: mean match distance on errors is 2.84 m against 3.12 m on correct calls, so the placement error is lateral onto a different feature, not far away. What this scorecard does not say

  • Nothing here is a count. Every matrix conditions on a matched pair. 4,496 destroyed DINS records and 2,783 destroyed Carlson points never enter one. The count-level answers are spec §B (structures) and §C (the 0.764 ratio).
  • Eaton and Palisades are 83 % of the DINS evidence, and Camp, Woolsey and Thomas are 40 % of the Carlson evidence. The fire-level bootstrap intervals are wide because of that, and that width is the honest one.
  • Seven of the fifteen DINS fires are out of the bundle's training pool and hold 60 destroyed structures between them. Everything else is leave-one-fire-out, which is the strongest claim available and is not a time hold-out (goal-time-holdout).
  • One fire is not a state. Marshall carries every non-California row here. The real out-of-state evidence is val-generalization-outside-ca: over 25 non-California fires, mean-fire F1 0.7345 at the shipped cut against 0.8002 at each fire's own cut. The cut, not the ranking, is what does not travel.
  • The 2×2 is a choice. 250 DINS records in the 25–50 % band and 1,763 Affected/Minor records say so.
  • No number here changed anything. No default, threshold, bundle, column or config value moved.

.venv/bin/python scripts/26_damage_scorecard.py --allow-real-data-writes

CPU only, single process, about a minute — the fire-level bootstrap is 2,000 replicates of a matrix product, not 2,000 refits. --stage {dins,carlson,external,taxonomy,curves} runs one part. The crop grids are a separate, network-bound step through scripts/qa_audit_crops.py.

Which buildings each source finds

This is one wooded neighbourhood in the 2024 Park Fire (Tehama County, California). After the fire, CAL FIRE inspectors recorded the buildings here and graded their damage; the coloured dots are their records. Drawn over the same picture are two public building-footprint maps and the extra buildings our roof detector found, so you can see which buildings each source knows about. Zoom out or press “Whole fire” to see the rest of the fire.

    What to look for

    • Buildings no footprint map has. In the opening view, three buildings the inspectors recorded have no footprint in either USA Structures or the Microsoft map, but our roof detector found them (gold diamonds): two of them were destroyed and one was undamaged. Across the whole fire, 94 inspection points have a detector point within 10 m and no footprint within 10 m.
    • Buildings nobody mapped. Four inspection points in the opening view have no footprint and no detector point within 10 m. Three are small outbuildings, which are hard to see under trees in a 60 cm aerial photo. Across the whole fire (within 300 m of the perimeter), 854 of 2,064 inspection points have nothing from any of the three sources within 10 m; 456 of those are minor structures such as sheds, and 373 are homes.
    • The two footprint maps disagree. Some roofs have a blue USA Structures outline only, some a purple Microsoft outline only, and some both, offset by a few metres. The two maps were traced from different photos taken in different years.
    • Destroyed buildings still have footprints. Footprints and the detector are drawn from photos taken before the fire, so they show what stood there, not what survived. Damage comes from the inspection grade (the dot colour).
    • An outline with no inspection dot is not proof of a phantom. Inspectors record buildings inside the damage area; an outline without a dot may be a real building they did not visit, or one that was already gone. We did not find a clear phantom footprint in the opening view.

    Distances such as “within 10 m” are measured from the inspection point to the nearest footprint edge or detector point; inspection points are sometimes placed a few metres off the roof, so these counts are approximate.

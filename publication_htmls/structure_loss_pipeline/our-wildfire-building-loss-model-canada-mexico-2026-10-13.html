Wildfire structure loss · outside the U.S.

Our wildfire building-loss model in Canada and Mexico · 2026-10-13

Jasper 2024 (Alberta; town assessment, internal), McDougall Creek and Bush Creek East 2023 (British Columbia; reported totals) and the October 2019 Baja California fires (Mexico; reported totals); building lists from Overture + Microsoft footprints (2026); first damage call only; compared with 40 U.S. fires never trained on; plus how complete today's building maps are for older fires

By Carlo Broderick, NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara · 2026-10-13 19:01 PDT

The model that finds destroyed buildings after U.S. wildfires uses only Sentinel-2 satellite images, which cover the whole world, so it runs unchanged in Canada and Mexico. What it lacks abroad is our U.S. building finder (it needs U.S. aerial photos) and public building-by-building damage records to check it against. On the one Canadian fire with such a record, Jasper 2024 (370 destroyed buildings), it found and called destroyed 71% of them, against 61% on U.S. fires it never trained on.

How to read this page

Bars compare our model abroad with the same model on U.S. fires it never saw. Hover any bar or cell for the counts behind it. 'Destroyed' means the building was more than half destroyed. This is a scientific test, not an official damage assessment.

How we could rebuild what stood before an older fire

  • Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal (yearly building maps 2016–2023, CC BY 4.0): covers our Mexican frame (8 yearly images) but not Canada (0 images at Jasper, McDougall Creek, Bush Creek East). Tested on the Baja 2019 burned area: the estimated buildings inside the area divided by those in a 0.5–3 km ring around it (per cent, by year): 2016 9.9, 2017 9.8, 2018 9.5, 2019 9.8, 2020 9.7, 2021 9.8, 2022 9.9, 2023 10.2. A loss of about 211–236 homes among some 19,000 buildings is a 1% dip, smaller than the year-to-year wobble, so the test cannot tell; it may still work for fires that destroyed a large share of a town.
  • Microsoft footprints: made from imagery of 2014–2024 (dates were not read building by building in this test); Microsoft's separate Canadian release (2019) predates the 2021+ Canadian fires and can serve as a before-fire map.
  • Statistics Canada's Open Database of Buildings records the source and year of each municipal layer: a dated pre-fire map where a town contributed one.
  • Built-up maps from satellites (World Settlement Footprint Evolution, GHSL, Dynamic World 'built' class): 10–30 m cells, can show whether a block was built before a fire, not single houses.
  • Our own Structure Inventory Model logic: drop map buildings that first appear after the fire (Overture changelog 'added' dates, Microsoft image dates) and add back destroyed-not-rebuilt buildings seen in the before-fire Sentinel-2 composite; the detector itself needs NAIP, so abroad this would use footprint maps plus satellite checks.
  • For fires from 2023 on, Overture can be pinned at the release before the fire (we keep its changelog), which the Jasper panel shows works.
How this panel was checked
  • Only the Google temporal option was tested here (Baja 2019); the others are a plan.

5 · What is likely to differ outside the U.S.

Differences to expect

  • Building styles: many cabins and small lake homes in B.C.; dense concrete-block homes in Baja California, which may leave more standing walls after a fire.
  • Snow and season: the model compares a before-fire and an after-fire window; northern fires can have snow in the yearly before-fire composite and short snow-free seasons after late-summer fires.
  • Boreal and mountain forest over roofs: dense conifers hide small roofs from a 10 m satellite.
  • Image dates: the date the fire reached the buildings is used as the start; for long fires (Bush Creek East burned for weeks before reaching Scotch Creek) this matters.
  • Building lists made after the fire: the biggest difference for past fires (panels above).
  • Wind: the gridded hourly wind (ERA5-Land, global) being added to the wind analysis can be used for Canadian and Mexican fires later.
How this panel was checked
  • Expectations from how the model works, not measured here except the building-list effect.

Harris fire 2007: did its Mexican block burn?

Our record holds 141 close-together buildings near Tecate, Mexico, inside the Harris fire outline. A Landsat burn-severity check (before vs after the fire) finds a burn signal around 0 of the 141 (median change 0.007), against 148 of 165 U.S. buildings the USGS record marks destroyed (median 0.20), and Baja California civil protection said on 24 October 2007 that the fire 'did not go far into Mexico', burning three cabins at Rancho La Puerta. This measures burned ground, not damage to each building.

How good are the point datasets we validate against?

Structure Loss Pipeline · QA · Carlson (USGS) and CAL FIRE DINS as structure-location truth

The short version

Carlson points sit at building-footprint centroids: where pre-fire USA Structures exists, the median Carlson point is about 3 m from the footprint centroid (3.1 m on Woolsey, 2.8 m on Creek), and they are near-complete for destroyed structures: 93.6 % of them on Camp, 95.1 % on Tubbs and 89.0 % on Thomas match a DINS destroyed inspection one-to-one at 20 m (Woolsey 75.9 %). DINS points are inspector-placed: typically 5–12 m from the footprint centroid, only about a third of them inside a footprint at all, and 16–47 % of their destroyed records (23 % on Camp) are minor structures — sheds, detached garages — that Carlson excludes by design.

Both are usable as structure-location truth at a 20 m match radius, and neither is usable at 5 m. Neither can be treated as complete under dense canopy, and Carlson contains points where nothing is visible in NAIP at all (Carlson used other imagery and footprint sources), which caps the recall any NAIP-only detector can ever reach. Finally, the pre-fire USA Structures label polygons are themselves incomplete — essentially absent in Sonoma before Tubbs and most of Ventura before Thomas, with visible omissions in Butte — so USA-Structures-only labels teach a network that real houses are background.

What each dataset is

  • Carlson et al. 2025 (USGS data release doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD) — one point per major building inside or near the perimeter of 2002–2022 US wildfires, coded B destroyed / U surviving. Compiled from building footprints and imagery, so the points are footprint centroids.
  • CAL FIRE DINS — damage inspection records, 2013–2025, one point per inspected structure, including minor structures. Placed by an inspector in the field, so the point marks where the record was taken, not the centre of the roof.
  • USA Structures (FEMA / ORNL) — the building footprint polygons the pipeline uses as training labels, filtered here to scenes extracted before each fire.

What this means for validating the pipeline

  • Match at 20 m, one to one. Not 5 m: the two reference datasets do not agree with each other at 5 m. One to one, because nearest-neighbour matching lets one predicted point claim a whole cluster of inspections.
  • Report recall against Carlson and against DINS. They disagree about what counts as a structure, and the gap between the two recalls is information, not noise.
  • Report DINS minor structures separately. 16–47 % of DINS destroyed records per fire are sheds and detached garages that Carlson never contained and that a footprint inventory will not have. Pooling them silently lowers recall.
  • Never call either dataset complete under canopy. Both the crop audit and the residual mismatches point the same way: dense tree cover over small homes is where both censuses and any 0.6 m detector lose buildings together.
  • Carlson has points with nothing visible in NAIP. That is a ceiling on NAIP-only detection recall, not a model failure — it belongs in the caveats of any recall number.

How to reproduce

uv run python scripts/qa_point_datasets.py --allow-real-data-writes
uv run python scripts/qa_audit_crops.py --points … --year 2018     --out grid.png

The first script writes tables.csv, metrics.json, manifest.json and this page under outputs/qa_point_datasets/; every number above is read from that metrics.json at build time. The second renders the NAIP crop grids and needs the network (Planetary Computer STAC + windowed COG reads); it is deliberately a separate script so this page can be rebuilt offline.

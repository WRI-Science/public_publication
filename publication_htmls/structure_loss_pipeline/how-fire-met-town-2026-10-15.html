Structure-loss paper · urban conflagrations

How the fire met the town · 2026-10-15

Fires whose biggest block of close buildings held more than 89: 8 of 23 Carlson 2002–22, 10 of 25 in our 2002–26 record and 7 of 14 CAL FIRE inspection incidents became urban conflagrations · eight measures of how the fire met the town (wind into the block, run before it, grass and shrub on the approach, night arrival, other fires at once, fuel dryness, ground slope, front width), all blocks of 50 or more (42 ours), 69 block and 3,236 patch units, 19 named fires

By Carlo Broderick, NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara · started 2026-10-14 19:00 PDT · updated 2026-10-14 23:02 PDT

Every urban conflagration so far needed a big block of close buildings (more than 89) and a regional wind event, yet only about 4 in 10 big-block fires in strong wind became one. Here we test what earlier work had not touched: how the fire arrived. None of the eight ways the fire met the town separate the fires that became urban conflagrations from the big-block fires that did not, in all three records. One hint (same direction in every record, clear in one): the ground the fire crossed in its last kilometre fell less steeply for those that became urban conflagrations (middle values -1.3% vs -5.5% in our record; many of the others were reached by fire running downhill off the hills above them). Groups are small (10 vs 15 fires in our record), so only large differences could show; 'not shown' is not 'no effect'. Described but not tested: in the fires that became urban conflagrations, the ground on the side the fire came from was less often vegetation and more often other buildings (middle value 41% vs 82% vegetation in our record), which suggests the fire often reached the block across other built-up land rather than straight out of the wildland.Pre-registered (docs/ledger.d/2026-10-15-0110-fire-meets-town--prereg.md): M1–M8 at the biggest block (35 m links), A = largest 30 m destroyed chain ≥ 50 vs B = biggest block > 89 without one; three full records; Holm over 8; continuous outcome over blocks ≥ 50; patch level with fire clusters. Descriptive only.

How to read this page

Urban conflagration: at least 50 destroyed buildings joined in one chain, each within 30 m (centre to centre) of another. Block: the biggest group of buildings standing before the fire, each within 35 m of another. Became / did not: fires whose biggest block held more than 89 buildings, split by whether they became an urban conflagration. Hover on any number, dot or label; switch to Technical for methods.

Does how the fire met the town separate the two groups?

None of the eight ways the fire met the town separate the fires that became urban conflagrations from the big-block fires that did not, in all three records. One hint (same direction in every record, clear in one): the ground the fire crossed in its last kilometre fell less steeply for those that became urban conflagrations (middle values -1.3% vs -5.5% in our record; many of the others were reached by fire running downhill off the hills above them). Groups are small (10 vs 15 fires in our record), so only large differences could show; 'not shown' is not 'no effect'. A caution on 'other large fires': the count came from the national situation report dated the arrival day, a morning snapshot that misses fires starting overnight in the same outbreak (Tubbs and Witch show none). With the next morning's report (a check added afterwards, not pre-registered) the big-block fires that did not become urban conflagrations sat amid more other large fires (middle percentile 97 vs 79 in our record; p 0.08, 0.03, 0.20 in the three records), the opposite of 'crews stretched thin' as the deciding difference.Two-sided Mann-Whitney (Fisher exact for night); medians; Holm over M1–M8 within each record; reading rule in docs/ledger.d/2026-10-15-0110-fire-meets-town--prereg.md §5.

Where the urban conflagration happened

In 8 of the 10 urban conflagrations in our record the biggest block itself lost most of its buildings. In Tubbs 2017 and Woolsey 2018 the biggest block was largely spared and the urban conflagration happened in another big block (red boxes). The largest destroyed chain lies outside the biggest block in 6 of 10, but often only because a 30–35 m gap splits a block that burned as a whole.

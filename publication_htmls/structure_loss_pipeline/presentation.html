How we find the buildings

Six steps turn public maps and aerial photos into one list of the buildings standing before the fire.

1

Pre-fire footprints

FEMA USA Structures and Microsoft building outlines, kept only if drawn from imagery taken before the fire.

2

Aerial photos

USDA NAIP: 0.6 m pixels in red, green, blue and near-infrared, from the latest flight before the fire.

3

Roof detector

A neural network (U-Net) paints how likely each pixel is a roof. Trained on 43,364 image tiles from 39 areas.

4

Keep new roofs only

Roof pixels over 0.5 form patches; each patch of 20 m² or more becomes a point, unless it sits within 3 m of a known footprint.

5

Merge and tag

Footprints and new roofs become one list, each point tagged by source and size (major = 50 m² or more, the USGS definition).

6

Score for damage

Only buildings on this list get damage calls. A building missing here is missing from the loss count.

Code: slp/inventory.py, scripts/04 · detector checkpoint the production detector · the public release omits Microsoft footprints for licence reasons

Step by step. 1 Footprints: USA Structures kept if IMAGE_DATE precedes ignition; Microsoft v2 kept if its capture window ends before the fire (undated polygons kept); a Microsoft point is added only if it is not inside an accepted footprint buffered by 3 m. 2 Imagery: 4-band NAIP at 0.6 m. 3 Detector: U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder; footprints are fed as a fifth band (randomly dropped in training) so it learns to fill gaps rather than redraw them; trained on 43,364 chips from 39 units (5 Carlson fires, WUI areas, 20 DINS fires 2019–22), split by 2.5 km spatial blocks 70/15/15. 4 Post-processing: probability cut 0.5, connected patches of at least 20 m², dropped if inside any footprint buffered by 3 m. 5 Tags: source (usa_structures, microsoft_v2, microsoft_2022, unet) and size class. 6 Evaluation truth: Carlson census points (recall and precision) and CAL FIRE inspections (recall only, because only inspected structures are listed), matched one-to-one within 10 m.

bands, burn indices, change vs its own pre-fire self, date

Transformer + CNN

over time, plus the image patch around the building

Trained honestly

Each fire's column comes from a network that never saw that fire

Augmented

Random brightness and timing shifts teach it to ignore haze and revisit gaps

Time-legal

For the ❶ first call it sees only the first 74 days of the fire it scores

Tested and dropped: bigger networks, pooling, recurrent encoders, label-noise losses, sun-angle inputs

Tokens are 12 reflectance channels (the building's own pixel and its 3x3 mean) plus six indices and their difference from the building's own pre-fire mean; up to 128 time slots with a sinusoidal date encoding. A small transformer reads the sequence; a small CNN reads the 3x3 patch (wider 5x5 and 7x7 patches are being tested on the full imagery). Stacking: the network outputs one probability per building, joined as a column into the gradient-boosted tree, which learns how much to trust it. Leave-one-fire-out end to end. Augmentation that won: radiometric jitter plus time jitter, without rotations (+0.0059 over with rotations, canonical). Headline: +0.076 per fire [+0.062, +0.091] for the full first call model over the 62-signal base. Both networks are saved and score every fire in the record. A net trained on randomly truncated histories (all horizons) was tested and gives no gain at 74 days (+0.002, interval spans zero); an earlier screen figure (+0.0325) was a caching error and is withdrawn.

F1: one score for misses and false alarms

Precision

Of the buildings we call destroyed, the share that really were. Low precision means false alarms.

Recall

Of the buildings really destroyed, the share we caught. Low recall means misses.

F1

A balance of the two. It is high only when both are high; 1.0 is perfect.

Worked example

90 caught

10 missed

5 false alarms

Precision 0.95

Recall 0.90

F1

0.92

Why not accuracy? Only about 7% of buildings are destroyed, so a model that calls every building "survived" is 93% accurate, yet its F1 is zero.

Every F1 in this deck: the destroyed call at 0.88, each fire held out. Green = counted over all buildings; orange = averaged per fire.

F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall: 2 x P x R / (P + R), which equals 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN). Harmonic rather than ordinary mean, so a model cannot hide weak recall behind strong precision or the reverse. Worked example: TP 90, FP 5, FN 10 gives precision 0.947, recall 0.900, F1 = 180 / 195 = 0.923. True negatives (surviving buildings correctly called survived) do not enter F1 at all, which is why it suits a rare outcome like destruction; accuracy is dominated by them. The calls depend on the threshold: a building is called destroyed when its probability is 0.88 or above, and moving that cut trades precision against recall. Pooled F1 counts every building in one pile; per-fire F1 computes F1 for each fire and averages, so a small fire counts as much as Camp. AUC, shown later, is threshold-free.

Why no model can score 1.0: the label ceiling

Where Carlson and CAL FIRE both assessed the same buildings, they disagree on about 2% of them.

We grade our model against those labels. When the label itself is wrong, a correct call is scored as a mistake.

So even a perfect model tops out below 1.0. That cap is the ceiling.

How we know: 43,929 buildings were assessed by both teams. A statistical model reads their pattern of agreement across 14 fires and estimates each team's error rate, without assuming either one is right.

Estimated with a latent-class (Hui-Walter) model that infers each source's error rates without assuming either is right: CAL FIRE sensitivity 0.995 / specificity 0.984; Carlson 0.986 / 0.990. ODbL's share-alike clause would pass to every user of the whole dataset. We dropped the 66,265 Microsoft-derived rows (2.85% of the release, 301 fires; 36% of CZU, 69% of Tiger Island).

Options we weighed

A · license everything ODbL (rejected) · B · drop the rows (chosen) · C · treat points as a 'produced work' that needs only attribution (recommended technically; needs a legal ruling).

For the team to decide

Get a ruling on option C to restore the rows · mint a DOI · is using Microsoft footprints to train the detector acceptable? · ask the Red Cross about partner access.

Decision of 11 Sep 2026 (licence-decision.md) · the cost of option B: the release is least complete on rural fires, where footprint databases are weakest

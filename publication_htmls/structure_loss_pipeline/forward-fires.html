Structure Loss Pipeline · results

Fire by fire

Every fire this pipeline has produced a per-structure loss call for, and the inspection record it was scored against. Fifteen California fires of 2024–2025 make up the published v0.3.0 series; two more sit outside it, one outside California and one inside the Carlson era, because they have truth sets nobody in this project drew.

What these numbers are, 12 September 2026

Every row is the shipped v0.3.0 product: inventory seg_all_v4 on each fire's own perimeter frame, damage bundle damage_sentinel2s1_v4, fixed cut 0.87, after the road-10 overlap-attribution rule. Eight of the fifteen fires are in that bundle's own training pool and none of them is scored by it — each carries a leave-one-fire-out probability from a model refitted on the other 73 fires, marked lofo below. The optical-only bundle and the per-fire operating rule the owner adopted on 2026-09-11 are still being built and no row here is scored by them.

the product, scored

Seventeen fires

Matching is greedy one-to-one at 10 m between our inventory points and the truth records, so a call is only scored where both datasets found the same building. Detection recall is the share of truth records our inventory matched; P / R / F1 are for the destroyed class on the matched pairs only. A detection miss is therefore not counted as a damage error — it is counted in the detection column, where it belongs.

Reading the two low rows

Franklin 2024 was an attribution failure, not a ranking failure. It scored F1 0.240 at AUC 0.948 in v0.2.0 — 333 predicted destroyed against 20 inspected as destroyed. The AUC is the tell. Franklin ignited near Malibu on 9 December 2024; the Palisades fire burned 13.0 km² of Franklin's Indirect ring on 7 January 2025, day 29 of Franklin's own 74-day post-fire window, and 297 of Franklin's 333 destroyed calls sat inside the Palisades perimeter. The model read the pixels correctly and filed them under the wrong fire. Since v0.3.0 an attribution rule cedes those calls to the later fire: Franklin is now 35 predicted destroyed and F1 0.300, and 877 of its rows carry no damage call at all, with damage_null_reason = overlapped_by_DINS_2025_PALISADES saying why.

Truncating Franklin's window at the Palisades ignition was built, applied and rejected: the truncated window is 14 days holding one Sentinel-2 scene against the full window's six, median post-fire observations drop 5 → 1, and two of Franklin's three correct destroyed calls are lost. A 14-day composite is a different quantity from the 60-day one the bundle was fitted on, not a shorter look at the same one.

Coches 2 2025 is a small-numbers row. 143 inspections, 9 destroyed, 118 matched pairs, 2 called destroyed — precision 1.000 and recall 0.200. It is also the fire where the public footprint database is best (baseline recall 0.825) and where the detector adds nothing at all. One fire of this size moves a mean-per-fire F1 by several points on its own, which is why the pooled row and the per-fire mean are both published.

The two fires that are not in the series

South Fork (NM, June 2024) is the only verified post-2022 per-structure damage assessment outside California — FEMA Urban Search & Rescue observations published by the Village of Ruidoso. It is scored on the assessment hull (39.7 km²), not the whole fire, because an assessment is not a census and enumerating over the whole 17,569-acre fire would make every uninspected building a false positive by construction. Its detection recall (0.661) is low for structural reasons: New Mexico has no cached Microsoft footprint layer, so the detector's prior band is USA Structures alone, and Lincoln County's USA Structures extract carries a single IMAGE_DATE of 2017-05-26 — seven years before the fire.

Marshall (CO, December 2021) is in Carlson's era, not the forward one, so it is not in the release. It is here because it is the only fire in this project with a public per-structure assessment nobody in this pipeline drew — two of them, in fact, plus Carlson's own points. Marshall also answered the winter question. Its pre-fire window gives a median of 6 clear Sentinel-2 observations per structure and its post-fire window a median of 2, against 9–17 on a summer California fire — and even so only 12 of 14,786 structures (0.08 %) could not be scored. Snow does not stop it; the post composite is simply thin.

the per-fire maps

Look at a fire

Each map is one self-contained page: the pre-fire inventory drawn on satellite imagery, coloured by predicted destroyed probability, with the inspection record beside it. Whole fires carry tens of thousands of points and will not fit in one browsable page, so each map draws full detail inside the densest 800 m neighbourhoods plus the cell holding the most model misses outside them — the interesting failure — and covers the rest of the fire with a coarse 250 m rate grid. The sampling is stated on the map itself.

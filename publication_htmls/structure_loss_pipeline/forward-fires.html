Structure Loss Pipeline · results

Fire by fire

Every fire this pipeline has produced a per-structure loss call for, and the inspection record it was scored against. Fifteen California fires of 2024–2025 make up the 2023+ half of the published v0.4.0 series; two more sit outside it, one outside California and one inside the Carlson era, because they have truth sets nobody in this project drew.

What these numbers are, 12 September 2026

Every row is release v0.4.1: inventory seg_all_v4 on each fire's own perimeter frame, damage bundle damage_sentinel2_v5 — 62 optical features, no radar — at the bundle's own fixed cut 0.85, after the overlap attribution rule. Eight of the fifteen fires are in that bundle's own training pool and none is scored by it: each carries a leave-one-fire-out probability from a model refitted on the other fires, marked lofo.

A second call ships beside the first. damage_class is the fixed-cut call and is what every headline on this site is reported at. damage_class_adaptive is the same score cut by a per-fire rule that reads only that fire's own score distribution. Since v0.4.1 that rule is scoped to the Direct zone (expected_f1_isotonic_direct) — it takes the fire's threshold from the perimeter-plus-100 m population, whose base rate is nearer the one the rule was validated on, and applies it to every row. The adaptive column is advisory: on these fifteen fires it is worse on F1 and better on count, and the reason is worth reading before using it — see the two cuts below.

the product, scored

Seventeen fires

Matching is greedy one-to-one at 10 m between our inventory points and the truth records, so a call is only scored where both datasets found the same building. Detection recall is the share of truth records our inventory matched; P / R / F1 are for the destroyed class on the matched pairs only. A detection miss is therefore not counted as a damage error — it is counted in the detection column, where it belongs.

The two cuts, and why the fixed one is still the headline

A per-fire cut rule cleared a five-criterion promotion bar that was written down before it was scored, twice — unnested and fully nested over 2,211 model fits. The owner adopted it. Then it was applied to the product and it did not transfer, and the reason is not a flaw in the rule or in the bar.

The rule reads the fire's own score distribution and nothing else — that is what makes it label-free and what keeps it inside the covariate rule. It is also what makes it move when the population underneath it changes. On a table of inspected structures roughly half of which burned, the expected-F1 cut sits near the shipped one. On a whole inventory where nine structures in a hundred burned, the same arithmetic pulls the cut down — as far as 0.158 on Coches 2 — and the model starts calling structures destroyed that no inspector ever looked at.

⚠️ And what the adaptive call buys is the count. Over the fifteen fires' whole inventories it calls 15,951 structures destroyed against the fixed cut's 14,504 and DINS's 17,902 — a ratio of 0.810 → 0.891. So the two columns disagree in a specific, legible way: the fixed cut is the better classifier per structure and the adaptive cut gets the total much closer. Which one a reader wants depends on whether they are asking about a building or about a fire, and that is exactly why both ship. The release notes put it in one line: "use damage_class to classify buildings; use damage_class_adaptive if you are counting them" — and say which column you used. Every headline on this site is the fixed cut.

⚠️ That question has now been answered, and the answer is no. Roadmap 14 re-ran the same five criteria at the same thresholds on the product's own population — three populations, five candidate variants, the bar pre-registered again before anything was scored — and no candidate clears it. Nothing changes; the fixed cut stays the default. The best of them is the Direct-zone-scoped cut, which takes the rule's threshold from the Direct zone (base rate nearer the fold table's) and applies it to every row: mean-fire F1 −0.0186 [−0.0336, −0.0070] against the fixed cut, a destroyed-count ratio of 0.891, realised cuts 0.601–0.792 instead of 0.158–0.792 — and it gains outside California, 0.8986 → 0.9121 mean-fire F1. It was swapped in on 2026-09-12 and is the advisory column this page now reports — v0.4.1, a PATCH whose damage_class is byte-identical to v0.4.0's, so only the advisory column moved; its per-fire cuts were verified against the Stage 24 table on 15 of 15 fires.

One check is worth keeping. Re-fitting the isotonic map leave-one-fire-out — the leak a reader would reasonably suspect — produces a variant that is bit-identical on 204,797 of 204,797 inventory rows, because the product already reads each held-out fire's own fold calibration. Scored rather than asserted.

Reading the two low rows

The two fires that are not in the series

⚠️ South Fork has no advisory call at all. The Direct-scoped rule needs a Direct zone, and South Fork is the one product still on the legacy DINS-buffer frame — it has no perimeter, so no Direct ring, so the rule falls back to the fixed cut and the two columns are the same number. Its adaptive cell reads "—" rather than repeating the fixed one. (The 0.8825 this page carried before v0.4.1 was the whole-inventory rule, which no longer ships.)

South Fork (NM, June 2024) is the only verified post-2022 per-structure damage assessment outside California — FEMA Urban Search & Rescue observations published by the Village of Ruidoso. It is scored on the assessment hull (39.691 km²), not the whole fire, because an assessment is not a census and enumerating over the whole 17,569-acre fire would make every uninspected building a false positive by construction. At v0.4.0 it scores P 0.982 / R 0.747 / F1 0.848 at the shipped cut on 1,637 matched pairs, AUC 0.985, against F1 0.883 under the adaptive cut and 0.945 at the fire's own best cut of 0.14 — a threshold gap of +0.097. Its detection recall (0.661) is low for structural reasons: New Mexico has no cached Microsoft footprint layer, so the detector's prior band is USA Structures alone, and Lincoln County's USA Structures extract carries a single IMAGE_DATE of 2017-05-26 — seven years before the fire.

Marshall (CO, December 2021) is in Carlson's era, not the forward one, so it is not in the release. It is here because it is the only fire in this project with a public per-structure assessment nobody in this pipeline drew — two of them, in fact, plus Carlson's own points. Marshall also answered the winter question. Its pre-fire window gives a median of 6 clear Sentinel-2 observations per structure and its post-fire window a median of 2, against 9–17 on a summer California fire — and even so only a handful of its 14,774 scored structures could not be called. Snow does not stop it; the post composite is simply thin.

the per-fire maps

Look at a fire

Each map is one self-contained page: the pre-fire inventory drawn on satellite imagery, coloured by predicted destroyed probability, with the inspection record beside it. Whole fires carry tens of thousands of points and will not fit in one browsable page, so each map draws full detail inside the densest 800 m neighbourhoods plus the cell holding the most model misses outside them — the interesting failure — and covers the rest of the fire with a coarse 250 m rate grid. The sampling is stated on the map itself.

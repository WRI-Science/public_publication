Structure Loss Pipeline · the dataset

One layer, two eras

The USGS Carlson building-loss record and this pipeline's per-fire products, harmonised onto one schema, one row per structure per fire — so a user reads a single file instead of joining two datasets and discovering the joins do not work. This page is what is inside release v0.4.0, and where the two halves are not equivalent.

Status, 12 September 2026

The published series is v0.4.0, cut 2026-09-12, and it is the first version that carries all of Carlson: 361 fires and 1,770,939 rows, 2002–2022, verbatim. Until now the series read the five per-fire extracts that happened to be cached, so a layer calling itself 2002-onward held five of Carlson's fires and began in 2017. With our fifteen fires the internal build is 1,939,136 rows over 376 fires; the released package is 1,922,889 after the licence exclusion below.

Three other things changed with it: the production damage model is optical-only (damage_sentinel2_v5, no radar) at a fixed cut of 0.85; a second loss call ships beside the first; and the licence is decided. in flight Roadmap 12 is still building eighteen retrospective 2016–2022 inventories, which are not series rows yet, and Roadmap 13 is not reported at all.

Carlson's 73,534 destroyed is the sum of its own hand B calls over all 361 fires and is not a model output. The 879 rows with no damage call are all Franklin 2024 rows ceded to Palisades 2025 by the overlap-attribution rule, each carrying damage_null_reason = overlapped_by_DINS_2025_PALISADES.

Every row carries series_source (carlson or slp), an era whose boundaries are sensor launch dates rather than preferences, harmonised damage fields, per-row provenance, and the uncertainty its era can support: ours carries destroyed_prob; Carlson's is explicitly null rather than faked. A field only one era can fill is null on the other side and is never back-filled.

The 2023+ half is one detector on one frame with one damage bundle — seg_all_v4, the perimeter frame, damage_sentinel2_v5 at 0.85 — and the build manifest computes that rather than asserting it: heterogeneity_warning is null. prob_source says on every row whether the call came from the production bundle scoring a fire it had never seen (production, 32,997 rows) or from a leave-one-fire-out refit because the fire sits in that bundle's own training pool (lofo, 135,200 rows). An in-sample score is never shipped.

⚠️ Two things published about this table have since been corrected, and both corrections are here.

(1) "Every fire above ~7 % destroyed lands between 0.82 and 1.06 of Carlson's count" is false on 23 fires. Roadmap 12 put every California Carlson fire of 2016–2022 through four admission tests and took eighteen, spanning all seven years; on 23 fires and 394,460 buildings the same code gives agreement 0.979, κ 0.871, destroyed P/R/F1 0.903 / 0.863 / 0.883, AUC 0.991, and four of the twelve fires above 7 % fall outside the published band — two of them not by rounding: Creek 2020 at 1.13 and Zogg 2020 at 1.61. What survives is the direction (mean ratio 1.041 above 7 % against 0.720 below 2.5 %), and a fourth low-loss confirmation nobody had looked at: Kincade 2019, 1.7 % destroyed, ratio 0.67. Zogg 2020 is the worst fire in the era — AUC 0.897 against 0.975–1.000 everywhere else — and not for want of observations.

(2) The guidance was written on a bundle fitted through a sampler that read the wrong pixel for 85.1 % of rows. On the corrected tables the low-loss under-call is mostly gone: mean ratio below 2.5 % destroyed 0.720 → 0.952, Kincade 0.67 → 0.90, Creek 2017 0.64 → 0.91. And the apparent year trend mostly goes with it — the per-fire ratio correlates with fire year at Spearman +0.572 (p 0.004) on the old bundle and +0.310 (p 0.15) on the corrected one. It is not a Sentinel-2 constellation effect: median post-fire observations do not rise with the year (ρ +0.061). Every version of it points up, so a destroyed count that rises across 2022/2023 cannot be blamed on the model getting more conservative.

Two flags on Carlson's own release

The series carries Carlson verbatim. Nothing is dropped, merged, re-attributed or corrected: it is the USGS's release, and silently cleaning another team's published census inside a derived product would make the two impossible to reconcile. A user who counts rows in the carlson half gets exactly what they would get counting rows in the source shapefile. Two properties of that release will nevertheless break a naive count, so they are measured once over the whole national file and shipped as columns:

ColumnTrue whenRows
carlson_exact_duplicatethe row repeats an earlier row exactly — the same twelve published attributes and the same coordinates20,296 of 1,770,939
carlson_multi_fire_locationmore than one Carlson fire claims this location, at 0.5 m324,910 (18.3 %), at 152,537 distinct locations

Source: outputs/combined_series/manifest.json carlson_release_flags, measured over the whole national release before any subset is applied; docs/combined-series-spec.md §4c. Both null on every slp row, and since v0.4.0 both are written by a stage rather than being an ad-hoc read. The duplicate flag marks 20,296 rows in 40,580-row duplicate groups at 20,284 distinct duplicated rows; the multi-fire flag marks 324,910 rows at 152,537 of the release's 1,589,122 distinct locations.

One fire carries most of the duplication: CA_2008_SAYRE alone holds 19,859 of the 20,296 repeats — 40,146 rows at 20,287 locations. A national destroyed count that does not drop them is wrong by that much. The multi-fire flag marks every row at such a location, because without an attribution rule there is no "later" fire to prefer. De-duplicate on geometry before any pooled count across Carlson fires. These are flags, not fixes, and not a criticism: the duplicates are almost certainly an artefact of the 145-step select-then-merge lineage the release's own metadata records, and the multi-fire locations are a correct consequence of a definition — one row per (structure, fire) — that this series shares.

On the shipped layer the same exposure is now measurable on both halves. 328,786 of Carlson's 1,770,939 rows (18.6 %) and 9,264 of our 168,197 (5.5 %) sit where another fire also enumerated a structure, and 34 locations are coded destroyed under more than one fire — all of them on Carlson's side, because the overlap-attribution rule took ours to zero. (logs/CHANGELOG.md 2026-09-12 11:15 PT §3; outputs/forward_damage/overlap_series.json.)

The licence is decided: MIT for the code, CC BY 4.0 for the data, copyright Carlo Broderick. The PROVISIONAL markers are gone. The ODbL question is resolved by exclusion, not by argument: every row whose geometry came from a Microsoft footprint is dropped from the package — 16,247 of 1,939,136 rows (0.84 %), all of them on our fifteen fires, which is why the 2023+ half ships 151,950 rows rather than 168,197.

⚠️ The exclusion is uneven, and it is largest exactly where the footprint database is weakest. It removes 2.6 % of Airport's rows and 24.4 % of Bridge's and Nixon's. A user counting structures from the package on a rural fire is counting a filtered inventory; the internal build keeps those rows and every performance number this project reports about itself is computed on the full one.

Microsoft footprints were also used as a training prior for the detector, which is a different act from redistribution: the network reads a raster band saying a footprint database has a building here, and the weights and every structure the detector outputs are this project's own work. No Microsoft polygon, attribute or identifier is redistributed in any form.

⚠️ Upgrading from v0.3.0? Four things changed under you. Four columns were added (damage_class_adaptive, destroyed_threshold_adaptive, carlson_exact_duplicate, carlson_multi_fire_location); destroyed_threshold went from one constant to a per-fire value; the production bundle changed; and the Carlson structure_ids of four fires changed, because carlson_id is now enumerated per fire over the national release instead of over five cached extracts. CA_2018_WOOLSEY is unchanged only because it happened to be first. This is a MINOR under the major_zero rule and would be a MAJOR after v1.0.0.

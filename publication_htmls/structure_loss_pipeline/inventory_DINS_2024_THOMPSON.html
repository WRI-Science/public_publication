What is this map?

What you are looking at

This is the pipeline's product, run end to end on the Thompson Fire (2024 CAL FIRE incident) — a fire neither model has ever seen. Nothing about this incident was in any training set: not the buildings, not the imagery, not the year.

Two independent steps produced it. First, the pre-fire inventory: every USA Structures footprint standing before 2024-07-02, plus every building the U-Net found on 2022 NAIP aerial imagery that no USA Structures point explained — 5,467 plus 70 = 5,969 points. Second, the damage call: before / after sentinel2 reflectance at each of those points (2024-06-09 → 2024-06-09 before, 2024-08-21 → 2024-08-21 after) fed to the damage model, which calls 26 of them destroyed at a probability cut of 0.88.

The rings are the answer sheet: CAL FIRE DINS inspections for this incident, which arrived long after the models were trained. Red ring = the inspector found the building destroyed; green ring = it survived; grey ring = inaccessible, so nobody knows. A DINS ring with no inventory point inside it is a building the pipeline never knew existed; a red ring around a green dot is a building it thought survived.

Plain-language glossary

  • Carlson — independent building points from Carlson et al. 2025 (USGS data release doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Every point is a building assessed after a U.S. wildfire, 2002–2022. These are evaluation data: they were never used to train the building-detection model.
  • B / U — Carlson's damage codes. B = destroyed / burned; U = surviving / unburned.
  • Chip — a small square aerial photo (512×512 pixels, about 0.6 m per pixel) cut from USDA NAIP imagery. The detection model is trained and run on these tiles.
  • Found / missed — a Carlson point is found when a predicted structure point falls within 20 m of it, and missed otherwise. 20 m is the pipeline's matching radius (validate.match_radius_m).
  • In label — the Carlson point sits inside a USA Structures polygon, i.e. inside a building the model was actually taught to find. Points outside every label are buildings the training labels never showed the model.
  • Precision — of the buildings the model predicted, the share that match a Carlson point. Recall — of Carlson's buildings, the share the model found. F1 — their harmonic mean, one number that punishes being bad at either.
  • Out-of-fold / leave-one-fire-out — every fire is scored by a model trained on the other fires only, so a number for a fire never comes from a model that saw that fire.
  • TP / FP / FN / TN — on the damage maps: predicted destroyed and was (TP), predicted destroyed but survived (FP), predicted surviving but was destroyed (FN), predicted surviving and survived (TN).

Inventory — USA Structures

What you see: one dot per building the inventory got from USA Structures, 5,467 of them, thin white ring. The fill is the predicted probability the building was destroyed: green near 0, yellow in the middle, red near 1, grey where nothing could be scored. Hover for the probability, the occupancy class, the footprint area, and how many clear satellite scenes existed before and after the fire — a point scored from three scenes deserves less trust than one scored from twelve.

Source: FEMA / ORNL USA Structures, filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before ignition. Post-fire footprint products are never used: destroyed buildings are missing from them by construction.

Inventory — U-Net only

What you see: the 70 buildings the U-Net found on NAIP that no USA Structures point explained, drawn with a loud cyan ring. These are the pipeline's own additions and the reason step 1 exists at all — turn the USA Structures layer off to see them alone.

How to read them: a cyan ring sitting on a rooftop with no white ring nearby is a real gain. A cyan ring on a driveway, a pool, or a shipping container is a false positive. Both are here; the map does not filter them.

Source: the U-Net checkpoint run over whole NAIP items, vectorised, then thinned to points more than 20 m from any USA Structures centroid.

CAL FIRE DINS (the answer sheet)

What you see: hollow rings — one CAL FIRE damage inspection record. Red = Destroyed (>50%); green = any lesser damage class including No Damage; grey = Inaccessible, held separately because an inspector who could not reach a parcel is missing data, not a surviving building. Counted here: 26 destroyed, 159 not destroyed.

Source: raw/dins/dins_all.gpkg, filtered on INCIDENTNAME. DINS is evaluation only and was never used to build the inventory or fit the damage model.

Fire-wide grid

What you see: the whole unit summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the share of inventory points in that cell the model calls destroyed at a probability cut of 0.88 — pale yellow low, dark red high, grey where a cell holds fewer than three points. Every inventory point is in exactly one cell, so this layer is the honest fire-wide view that the detail windows cannot give you.

Damage call — refined (a second model, ~a year later)

What you see: the product's second damage call, drawn as a dashed violet ring on the same 5,969 of 5,969 points (100.0%) that carry one. The fill is the same green→yellow→red probability ramp, so the ring tells you which model and the fill tells you how sure. The layer is off by default: the primary call is what this product ships.

⚠️ These are two estimates, not one estimate and its correction. The primary estimate (threshold 0.88) is made about two and a half months after the fire starts. The refined estimate (threshold 0.84) needs a yearly satellite summary of the landscape that is published roughly a year after the fire burns — so it does not exist while a fire is current, and it is more accurate where it does exist. The two thresholds sit on two different models' score axes and are not comparable with each other.

In the released series these are the damage_class and damage_class_refined columns. Classify with the primary one, and say which you used; never build a single total out of both.

On this fire the refined model called 27 structures destroyed against the primary call's 26.

How much of the fire you are seeing

This fire has 5,969 points. Drawing them all would make a file no browser opens, so the map shows two things at two scales:

  • Detail windows — 7 squares of 800 m: the 6 with the most points plus the square with the most model failures outside them. Everything inside them is drawn point by point (2,017 points in total). Use the Jump buttons to move between them.
  • Fire-wide grid — every point in the fire summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the rate in that cell. No point is left out of the grid; the cell size grows automatically if a fire would need more than 6,000 cells.

Read the two together. A perfect damage model on an inventory that missed a third of the buildings is not a working product.

One inconsistency worth knowing: the map colours and the “called destroyed” count use the inventory's own destroyed_pred at a cut of 0.88 (26 points). The precision / recall / F1 in summary.json's damage_on_matched block imply a lower cut, so those two numbers are not on the same operating point. AUC is threshold-free and unaffected.

Where the data come from

  • Carlson — Carlson et al. 2025, USGS national structure-loss points 2002–2022 (doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Evaluation only.
  • USA Structures — Oak Ridge National Laboratory / FEMA building outlines (CC BY 4.0), filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before the fire's ignition. The stage-1 training labels.
  • NAIP — USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, 0.6 m 4-band, via the Microsoft Planetary Computer. What the detection model reads.
  • Sentinel-2 L2A / Landsat Collection 2 L2 — before / after reflectance at each point, via the Planetary Computer. What the damage model reads.
  • CAL FIRE DINS — damage inspection records, the out-of-sample check for fires after Carlson ends.
  • Basemap — Esri World Imagery / Street / Topographic tiles (needs a network connection; nothing else on this page does).

The map draws in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326). All distances, cell sizes and matching were computed in projected metres (the Carlson USA Contiguous Albers Equal Area frame) before reprojection.

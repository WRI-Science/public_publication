Structure Loss Pipeline · deep dive

What burned, and what was there first

A reproducible pipeline that enumerates the structures standing before a wildfire and then predicts which of them were destroyed, from public data only — so the USGS Carlson structure-loss record (2002–2022) can be continued to the present. 01 · the product

What the product is, and who it is for

The USGS Carlson structure-loss dataset is a per-building census of what wildfires destroyed in the conterminous United States between 2002 and 2022 — 361 fires, 1,770,939 rows, hand digitised. It is the reference layer for anyone modelling wildfire exposure, and it stops at 2022.

This pipeline continues it. For a fire from 2023 onward it produces one row per structure inside the fire's frame, carrying whether the structure stood before the fire, where it sat relative to the perimeter, and a continuous probability that it was destroyed with a call at a published threshold. Carlson's rows and ours are published as one layer on one schema so a user reads a single file rather than joining two datasets and discovering the joins do not work.

Three audiences, in the order the design serves them. Researchers extending an exposure or risk model past 2022 — they need the two eras comparable, and the honest answer about where they are not. Agencies and insurers who want a per-structure loss layer for a fire nobody has inspected — they need the probability, not just the call. Anyone checking the work — every fire has a browsable map of its predictions on imagery, and every number on this page names the file it came from.

For the plain-language version of the method, read How the pipeline decides what was there, and what burned. This page assumes a reader comfortable with remote sensing and out-of-sample validation.

02 · method

The two parts, and why they are scored separately

Part one — the pre-fire inventory

A structure inventory for a fire is the union of three sources: FEMA/ORNL USA Structures footprints filtered to those whose IMAGE_DATE precedes ignition; Microsoft US Building Footprints v2 filtered on their own capture_dates_range; and a U-Net (ResNet-34 encoder) run over 0.6 m NAIP imagery of the whole frame. The network is trained on 24k–43k 512-px chips with USA Structures footprints burned as masks and spatial-block splits that no block crosses.

Two design choices carry most of the detector's value. From seg_all_v3 onward the network takes the footprint database itself as an input band — a prior that says "a public layer has a building here" — together with an ignore mask (mask value 2) around Carlson and DINS points that no footprint covers, so the loss is not penalised for finding a building nobody drew. The ignore mask alone lifted recall on buildings with no USA Structures label from 0.34 to 0.47 at unchanged precision; feeding the prior took overall Carlson-test recall to 0.814. The canonical detector is seg_all_v4 — five input bands, no dead inputs — and detector work is frozen there.

A detection is merged into the inventory under a polygon-union rule: a detector or Microsoft point is kept unless it falls inside an existing footprint polygon plus 3 m. The older 20 m centroid rule deleted every garage, shed and second dwelling near a house. Structures below 50 m² of footprint are tagged size_class = minor and kept, not dropped, because Carlson counted major buildings only and pooling the two silently lowers recall.

The primary artefact is the 0.6 m structure-probability raster per fire (uint8 0–250, 255 = not inferred); points are cut from it, so a different threshold is a re-cut rather than a re-inference.

Part two — the damage call

At every inventory point, before/after reflectance is sampled from Sentinel-2 L2A (10 m, 2016 onward) and Landsat Collection 2 L2 (30 m, all years) over composites that open before ignition and close 74 days after it. 62 optical features per structure: the point pixel and its 3×3 neighbourhood mean, pre, post and delta, for NBR, NDVI, NIR and SWIR among others. What carries the signal is post − pre NBR and its neighbourhood mean (Cohen's d ≈ −2.4 on Camp), the NIR drop and the SWIR rise.

The learner is histogram gradient boosting, fitted on Carlson's hand B/U labels over 66 Sentinel-2 fires plus eight CAL FIRE DINS incidents — 74 fires, 618,826 rows — and scored leave-one-fire-out: fit on every other fire, predict the held-out one, pool the out-of-fold predictions. The production bundle is damage_sentinel2_v5 at threshold 0.85, chosen by best_mean_fire_f1.

Sentinel-1 radar was in this bundle and is not any more. Nine C-band gamma0 columns were promoted into production in September 2026 under a rule written in advance. On the corrected feature tables they no longer clear it — re-measured over four seeds, +0.0012 short on pooled F1 and losing Bridge by −0.0087 against a 0.005 tolerance — and on 2026-09-11 the owner dropped them. Removing them costs nothing measurable on the product: holding the cut fixed and changing only the model is −0.0039 mean-fire F1, 95 % CI [−0.0106, +0.0025]. It also removes a dependency — the optical bundle can be run on a fire from before the radar archive opened in October 2014, which the radar bundle could not. The archived bundles stay on disk.

⚠️ The one rule that governs what may be a feature. No attribute of the fire itself — size, perimeter, dNBR, region, state, year — ever reaches the model, at fitting or at application (goal-no-fire-covariates). A test pins it by renaming a fire and moving it to another state and asserting nothing moves. Perimeters scope — they decide where to look and which exposure class a structure is in — and they never predict. This is what keeps the loss call from being a restatement of the fire's own attributes.

Why the two halves are never pooled into one score

An inventory error and a damage error are different mistakes against different truth sets. The inventory is judged against a census — Carlson enumerated every major building inside a fire's footprint area, so a missing building is measurable. The damage call is judged against inspections — CAL FIRE DINS visits damaged and threatened structures, so it can say whether a building we found burned but cannot say whether a building we found exists. Pooling them produces a number that answers neither question, and the two are reported apart everywhere on this page.

03 · scope

The frame, the exposure class, and the three eras

A fire's frame decides which structures the product enumerates. Since 2026-09-10 it is the fire's public perimeter (NIFC/WFIGS, with CAL FIRE FRAP as a fallback) buffered outward, matching Carlson's own rule: Direct is the perimeter plus 100 m, Indirect is 100 m to 2.4 km. The legacy frame — a 2.4 km buffer around CAL FIRE DINS inspection points — enumerated a different population by construction and could not define a fire at all outside California, where there are no DINS points.

Moving to the perimeter frame is the largest single change the product has made, and it was measured before and after on the same truth records:

measured against · FEMA/ORNL USA Structures ∪ Microsoft v2

Against the footprint databases

The public footprint databases are not a competitor. They are the baseline, and they supply most of every inventory this pipeline publishes. The only honest question is what the detector adds to them.

What the detector adds, against inspections

Stage 9b treats USA Structures ∪ Microsoft as the baseline and counts only the DINS records that the full inventory matches and the baseline does not. The match radius is 10 m, because the 10th-percentile nearest-neighbour spacing of DINS points is 8.7–11.3 m and a 20 m match can be the house next door.

Source: outputs/structure_metrics/seg_all_v4_full__perimeter__forward/; ledger val-stage9b-marginal, goal-footprints-are-baseline. Pooled interval from outputs/uncertainty/detector_marginal/.

The honest size of the contribution: 343 structures across fifteen fires — 0.9 points of recall, and 3.5 % of what the footprint database missed, 95 % CI [0.025, 0.066] over fires. At 20 m it is 394 of 8,632, 4.6 %. The footprints are the product, and we are slightly better than them.

The reason the database misses what it misses is not construction after the imagery. Of the 9,845 records it missed at 10 m, 8,208 have both a plausible YEARBUILT and a dated USA Structures footprint within 250 m, and only 85 of those 8,208 (1.0 %) were built after that footprint's IMAGE_DATE. The misses are minor structures — 5,279 of 9,845 are DINS "Other Minor Structure" — and buildings the digitiser did not draw.

What the detector adds, against a census

DINS cannot measure whether what we hold is real, because it only records structures somebody inspected. Carlson can: inside a fire's coverage it is a complete enumeration of major buildings, destroyed and surviving. Stage 9c scores both directions on the five Carlson fires with a per-fire extract on disk.

The footprint database is precise and incomplete. 0.858 of what it puts inside Carlson's coverage is a Carlson building (0.901 away from the coverage edge), and it holds 0.571 of Carlson's buildings. Recall here is a county-coverage number, not a detector one — Ventura's and Sonoma's pre-fire USA Structures vintages leave Thomas and Tubbs at 0.25–0.28, while Woolsey and Creek sit at 0.84–0.87. And the database misses destroyed buildings much harder than surviving ones (0.407 against 0.590 pooled), which is exactly the direction that biases a burn-probability model, so every published number stays split by bldg_cond.

On held-out spatial blocks only — the chips the detector never trained on, 38,821 Carlson buildings — adding the U-Net to the baseline is worth +4.1 recall points for 1.9 points of precision (0.768 → 0.809 recall, 0.851 → 0.832 precision at 10 m; 0.785 → 0.828 at 20 m). That is four times the marginal Stage 9b measures against DINS, and the reason is what the two truth sets are: Carlson enumerates every major building, DINS only the inspected ones, so most of what the detector adds is invisible to DINS by construction. This is the first evidence that the detector's additions are mostly real.

The boundary artefact is four times the scope artefact, and neither is what the raw 14.8 % invites a reader to think. The 50 m² line also under-corrects, and CAL FIRE says by how much: 608 of the 23,594 candidates have a DINS record within 10 m, 536 of them major-sized — and 112 of those 536 are DINS "Other Minor Structure", median area 74 m². Those are real minor buildings above the area cut that no area rule can find. Read 0.094 as the tightest upper bound we can defend on footprints that are not there, not as a measurement of them.

Nothing is removed. No operating point of the phantom signal gets removal precision above 0.36, so roughly two removals in three would delete a real building. The phantom score ships as a triage column for a human reviewer, and the review is not feasible at scale: the full queue for five fires is 23,594 polygons, 66 person-days at one polygon a minute. One analyst-week buys the ~1,800 polygons a prior-free detector pass is most suspicious of — a sample for estimating the rate, not a cleaning pass.

measured against · CAL FIRE DINS inspections

Against CAL FIRE DINS

DINS is the only per-structure truth set that covers fires after Carlson ends, so it is the product's primary out-of-sample check — and it is California only, which bounds everything it can say.

Detection: how many inspected structures the inventory found

Pooled over the 15 fires of 2024–2025, the full inventory matches 0.753 of the 38,497 DINS records one-to-one at 10 m and 0.786 at 20 m. Per fire this runs from 0.825 (Coches 2, suburban) to 0.432 (Park, rural Sierra) — the per-fire figures are the blue and ember marks in the chart above. Restricted to major structures, the Carlson-compatible population, suburban fires reach 0.85–0.95.

Precision is not measurable from DINS, and the page says so rather than reporting a number that looks like one. Of 2,267 U-Net points, 347 are confirmed against an inspection, 409 sit inside an inspected area and matched nothing, and 1,511 sit where nobody inspected. So at most 54 % of our additions inside an inspected area are wrong, and DINS cannot say more than that, because it records damaged and threatened structures, not every structure.

Damage: the loss call, per incident, out of sample

The eight 2024–25 DINS incidents are in the production bundle's own 74-fire training pool, so none of them is scored by it. Each carries a leave-one-incident-out probability from a model refitted on the other seven plus Carlson — same 71 features, same learner, same seed, same cut.

Each fold's own cut is recorded and not shipped. Fitted the production way on out-of-fold scores, the nine folds want 0.79–0.88 against the shipped 0.85. ⚠️ One fire in fifteen was a product defect, and the fix is a rule. Franklin 2024 ignited near Malibu on 9 December; the Palisades fire burned 13.0 km² of Franklin's Indirect ring on 7 January — day 29 of Franklin's own 74-day post window — and 297 of Franklin's 333 destroyed calls sat inside the Palisades perimeter. The AUC (0.948) was the tell: the ranking was fine and the attribution was not. Since v0.3.0 an attribution rule decides which fire may claim a structure two fires reached: Franklin went 333 → 35 predicted destroyed and F1 0.240 → 0.300, structures called destroyed under two fires went 271 → 0, and fourteen of fifteen fires were byte-for-byte unchanged. At v0.4.0 Franklin still ships 35 destroyed calls and scores F1 0.286, with 879 of its rows carrying no damage call at all and damage_null_reason saying why.

Expected calibration error is 0.0514 [0.0365, 0.0737] on the 66-fire national table and 0.0651 [0.0514, 0.1032] on the eight DINS incidents. the headline caveat · a rule that passed its bar and failed the product

Two cuts, and why the shipped one is still a fixed number

The single most important thing to understand about v0.4.0, and the reason it ships two loss calls instead of replacing one with the other.

Everywhere else in this project a threshold is a compromise: one number applied to every fire, costing the fires whose score distribution sits somewhere else. A label-free per-fire cut rule was built to fix that, scored leave-one-fire-out against a five-criterion bar pre-registered before the first fold, and it cleared all five — unnested, and again with the Carlson half fully nested over 2,211 model fits. The owner adopted it. That pass is real and nothing here contradicts it.

Then it was applied to the product, and it did not transfer. The reason is not a flaw in the rule or in the bar. It was validated on one population and applied to another.

The rule reads the fire's own score distribution and nothing else — that is what makes it label-free, and what keeps it inside the covariate rule. It is also what makes it move when the population underneath it changes. On a table of inspected structures where roughly half burned, the expected-F1 cut sits near the shipped one. On a whole inventory where nine in a hundred burned, the same arithmetic drags the cut down — as far as 0.158 on Coches 2 — and the model starts calling structures destroyed that nobody ever looked at.

⚠️ So the rule ships beside the fixed cut, not instead of it. damage_class is the fixed-cut call and is what every headline on this site reports; damage_class_adaptive and destroyed_threshold_adaptive carry the rule's. Both are on every scored row, both are in the data dictionary, and both are counted in the per-fire summary.

The pre-registered pass stands on the population it was measured on. Nothing about the bar was wrong: it answered a question about labelled folds and was read as an answer about the product. The open row names the next test rather than closing the question — the same five criteria evaluated on the product population, a Direct-zone-only variant whose base rate is nearer the fold table's and a whole-inventory variant, each pre-registered before it is run, as the original bar was (open-adaptive-rule-population-shift).

⚠️ That question has now been answered, and the answer is no. Roadmap 14 re-ran the same five criteria at the same thresholds on the product's own population — three populations, five candidate variants, the bar pre-registered again before anything was scored — and no candidate clears it. Nothing changes; the fixed cut stays the default. The best of them is the Direct-zone-scoped cut, which takes the rule's threshold from the Direct zone (base rate nearer the fold table's) and applies it to every row: mean-fire F1 −0.0186 [−0.0336, −0.0070] against the fixed cut, a destroyed-count ratio of 0.891, realised cuts 0.601–0.792 instead of 0.158–0.792 — and it gains outside California, 0.8986 → 0.9121 mean-fire F1. It was swapped in on 2026-09-12 and is the advisory column this page now reports — v0.4.1, a PATCH whose damage_class is byte-identical to v0.4.0's, so only the advisory column moved; its per-fire cuts were verified against the Stage 24 table on 15 of 15 fires.

One check is worth keeping. Re-fitting the isotonic map leave-one-fire-out — the leak a reader would reasonably suspect — produces a variant that is bit-identical on 204,797 of 204,797 inventory rows, because the product already reads each held-out fire's own fold calibration. And what the adaptive call buys is the count. Predicted destroyed over the fifteen fires' whole inventories goes 14,504 → 15,951 against 17,902 inspected as destroyed — a ratio of 0.810 → 0.891. So the two columns disagree in a legible way: the fixed cut is the better classifier per structure, and the adaptive cut gets the total right. Two more limits, stated because they bound every count comparison on this page. The assumption behind the caveat was wrong in sign, and it was worth checking rather than believing. Removing the leak was expected to shrink the adaptive rules' advantage; it grew it, by +0.0016. The effect is small either way, and the honest summary is that nesting was not what was holding this result up. What remains is the whole of the case against adopting: a narrow pass on 66 fires, 25 of them outside California, with several states represented by a single fire — and that is not a caveat a bigger computer can remove.

Why no single rule can win. The states split into two shapes and the label-free signal that fixes one wrecks the other. A rule that fits nothing at all — call the top mean(prob) of the fire's own scores — reaches Texas F1 0.623 against a labelled ceiling of 0.622, up from 0.109. The signal is genuinely there in the score distribution, with no labels and no fire attribute. It then costs California 13 points, because California's 41 fires are already at their cut. California is 41 of 66 fires, so a rule that helps eleven fires a lot and hurts forty a little can still post a positive mean — which is exactly what the California, per-state and worst-fire criteria exist to catch.

The defect that moved every damage number on this page

Stage 5 built its raster read window with rasterio.windows.from_bounds, which returns fractional offsets; src.read rounded them and src.window_transform did not, so the array and the transform that indexed it could disagree by a pixel — and how much padding the window carried decided which pixel a structure was read from. Worse, the six reflectance bands were read at a 3-pixel pad and the SCL cloud mask at a 2-pixel one, so a structure's cloud verdict could come from a different pixel than the reflectance it was masking. Both defects were confirmed on a raster whose digital number encodes its own row and column, fixed, and every Sentinel-2 feature table rebuilt.

10 · limits

Known limitations

  1. No forward hold-out exists yet. Every damage number is cross-validated or leave-one-fire-out; the production bundle has seen all eight DINS incidents. The fix is time. On 2026-09-10 the bundles, the cut and the composite windows were frozen and hashed, and a complete snapshot of the public CAL FIRE DINS archive taken the same day holds 132,522 records covering 2013–2025 and zero incidents of 2026. The same selection rule applied to 2025 picks exactly the 2025 fires already in the forward set, so the empty result is the archive, not the query. This project uses public data only, so the hold-out waits on CAL FIRE.
  2. Eight fires carry 84 % of the out-of-sample evidence in two of them. Every DINS interval on this page is a bootstrap over eight incidents, and Eaton and Palisades are most of the rows.
  3. The Indirect ring is not scoreable and carries the headline nowhere. On the Carlson table Indirect is 438,704 of 582,554 rows at a base rate of 0.0009 and the model scores F1 0.10 on them. Every headline in this project is carried by the Direct quarter of the table. On both outside-California fires the Indirect ring holds no destroyed structures at all.
  4. An inventory of building footprints undercounts DINS structures by roughly a fifth wherever several inspected structures sit on one parcel. A per-parcel or footprint-splitting step is the next thing to add.
  5. One DINS category should be excluded or scored separately. Infrastructure scores F1 0.286 (n 259, 16 destroyed): the model over-calls utility structures nine times out of ten, and the model and the inspector disagree about what "destroyed" means there.
  6. The intermediate DINS damage classes do not become separable. "Affected (>0–10 %)" scores mean 0.475 and "Minor (10–25 %)" 0.488, sitting almost exactly between No Damage (0.166) and Destroyed (0.933). Reading the score as a damage-severity index is defensible; reading it as "destroyed or not" puts a fifth of them over 0.9.
  7. Carlson's half has no attribution rule of its own. 18.3 % of its 1,770,939 rows sit at a location a second fire also claims, and its release has no structure identifier. De-duplicate on geometry before any pooled count across Carlson fires.
  8. The detector's additions cannot be proven right from DINS. At most 54 % of the U-Net points inside an inspected area are wrong, and DINS cannot narrow that, because it does not record every structure.
  9. Nothing before 2016 can be produced by this method at all, and nothing after 2022 has a Carlson enumeration to be checked against. The era comparison is bounded on both sides by construction.
  10. Footprints are never removed. The phantom rate is an upper bound and the triage ranking is a queue for a person, not a rule.

Three release gates have to clear with no override before a version is not a prerelease: no heterogeneity_warning, no damage bundle older than the configured production bundle, and no 2023+ fire without a damage call. v0.4.1 clears all three. ⚠️ It is a MINOR under the major_zero carve-out and would be a MAJOR after v1.0.0: four columns were added, destroyed_threshold went from one constant to a per-fire value, the production bundle changed, 356 fires were added, and the Carlson structure_ids of four fires changed because carlson_id is now enumerated per fire over the national release rather than over five cached extracts.

The USA Structures licence was re-verified against the publisher's own ArcGIS record on 2026-09-11 rather than against this project's notes.

The instruction was "as conservative as possible while letting anyone use it". CC BY 4.0 rather than CC0 because USA Structures is CC BY and we cannot dedicate to the public domain material we hold only under attribution; not ODbL, because that would make this dataset more restrictive than the Carlson record it continues, which is CC0. The packaging script drops every row whose detection_source is a Microsoft layer, recomputes the per-fire summary from what is left, and writes an exclusion table saying how many rows went, per fire. The internal build keeps them — it is a working directory, not a distribution, and every performance number this project reports about itself is computed on the full inventory.

⚠️ The exclusion is uneven and largest where the footprint database is weakest — 2.6 % of Airport's rows against 24.4 % of Bridge's and Nixon's — so a user counting structures from the package on a rural fire is counting a filtered inventory. The figure has moved as the pipeline improved: 5,701 rows at v0.1.0-rc1, 11,781 at v0.2.0-rc1, 16,247 now, because each fire came to search its whole frame.

⚠️ Microsoft footprints were also used as a training prior for the detector, which is a different question from redistribution: the network reads a raster band saying "a footprint database has a building here", and the weights and every structure the detector outputs are this project's own work. No Microsoft polygon, attribute or identifier is redistributed in any form.

Every figure on this page is transcribed from a file the pipeline wrote, named under the table that uses it. Where this page and an older document disagree, the file wins.

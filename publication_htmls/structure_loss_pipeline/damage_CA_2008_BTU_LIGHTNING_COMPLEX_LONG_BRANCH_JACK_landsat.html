What is this map?

What you are looking at

This map answers the pipeline's second question for the Btu Lightning Complex Long Branch Jack Fire (CA, 2008) (CA_2008_BTU_LIGHTNING_COMPLEX_LONG_BRANCH_JACK): of the buildings that stood before the fire, which ones did it destroy? Predictions are out-of-fold (leave-one-fire-out), from before / after landsat reflectance sampled at each building.

Each point is coloured by whether the model got it right and which way it was wrong at a probability threshold of 0.5: green correctly called destroyed (TP), blue correctly called surviving (TN), orange called destroyed but it survived (FP), red called surviving but it burned (FN). Hover any point for its predicted probability — a wrong call at 0.51 is a very different failure from a wrong call at 0.99.

Every probability on this map came from a model trained on the other fires only — leave-one-fire-out. Nothing here was fitted on CA_2008_BTU_LIGHTNING_COMPLEX_LONG_BRANCH_JACK. The learner shown is the one scripts/06_train_damage_model.py selected on pooled out-of-fold F1: random_forest.

Source: models/damage_landsat/oof_predictions.gpkg and metrics.json.

Plain-language glossary

  • Carlson — independent building points from Carlson et al. 2025 (USGS data release doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Every point is a building assessed after a U.S. wildfire, 2002–2022. These are evaluation data: they were never used to train the building-detection model.
  • B / U — Carlson's damage codes. B = destroyed / burned; U = surviving / unburned.
  • Chip — a small square aerial photo (512×512 pixels, about 0.6 m per pixel) cut from USDA NAIP imagery. The detection model is trained and run on these tiles.
  • Found / missed — a Carlson point is found when a predicted structure point falls within 20 m of it, and missed otherwise. 20 m is the pipeline's matching radius (validate.match_radius_m).
  • In label — the Carlson point sits inside a USA Structures polygon, i.e. inside a building the model was actually taught to find. Points outside every label are buildings the training labels never showed the model.
  • Precision — of the buildings the model predicted, the share that match a Carlson point. Recall — of Carlson's buildings, the share the model found. F1 — their harmonic mean, one number that punishes being bad at either.
  • Out-of-fold / leave-one-fire-out — every fire is scored by a model trained on the other fires only, so a number for a fire never comes from a model that saw that fire.
  • TP / FP / FN / TN — on the damage maps: predicted destroyed and was (TP), predicted destroyed but survived (FP), predicted surviving but was destroyed (FN), predicted surviving and survived (TN).

Wrong calls (FP + FN)

What you see: the model's mistakes. Orange = predicted destroyed, survived. Red = predicted surviving, destroyed. On by default, because the mistakes are what a reviewer needs to look at, and because they cluster: a red patch usually means one neighbourhood the before / after imagery could not see (smoke, snow, a long gap between clear scenes), not a scattered failure.

Correct calls (TP + TN)

What you see: green = correctly called destroyed, blue = correctly called surviving. Keep them on to judge whether the mistakes are a scatter through a mostly-right map, or a whole block the model got backwards.

Unscored points

What you see: grey — buildings with no usable prediction: 0 here. A point lands in this layer when its before / after features are incomplete (no clear scene either side of ignition) or its leave-one-fire-out fold was skipped. They are shown, and counted, rather than dropped — an accuracy number computed only on the easy points is not an accuracy number.

Fire-wide accuracy grid

What you see: the whole incident summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the share of points in that cell the model called correctly (red low, green high, grey where a cell has fewer than three points). Every point in the incident is in exactly one cell.

How much of the fire you are seeing

This fire has 5,610 points. Drawing them all would make a file no browser opens, so the map shows two things at two scales:

  • Detail windows — 7 squares of 800 m: the 6 with the most points plus the square with the most model failures outside them. Everything inside them is drawn point by point (1,535 points in total). Use the Jump buttons to move between them.
  • Fire-wide grid — every point in the fire summarised into 250 m cells, coloured by the rate in that cell. No point is left out of the grid; the cell size grows automatically if a fire would need more than 6,000 cells.

The AUC, precision, recall and F1 on the stats chip were written by the pipeline stage, not recomputed here. Precision, recall and F1 are at the 0.5 operating threshold; AUC is threshold-free, so it is the fairer single number when a fire's destroyed share is very high or very low.

Where the data come from

  • Carlson — Carlson et al. 2025, USGS national structure-loss points 2002–2022 (doi:10.5066/P1QX6UXD). Evaluation only.
  • USA Structures — Oak Ridge National Laboratory / FEMA building outlines (CC BY 4.0), filtered to scenes with IMAGE_DATE before the fire's ignition. The stage-1 training labels.
  • NAIP — USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, 0.6 m 4-band, via the Microsoft Planetary Computer. What the detection model reads.
  • Sentinel-2 L2A / Landsat Collection 2 L2 — before / after reflectance at each point, via the Planetary Computer. What the damage model reads.
  • CAL FIRE DINS — damage inspection records, the out-of-sample check for fires after Carlson ends.
  • Basemap — Esri World Imagery / Street / Topographic tiles (needs a network connection; nothing else on this page does).

The map draws in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326). All distances, cell sizes and matching were computed in projected metres (the Carlson USA Contiguous Albers Equal Area frame) before reprojection.

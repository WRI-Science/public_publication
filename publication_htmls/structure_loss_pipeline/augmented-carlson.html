Structure Loss Pipeline · the old record, upgraded

Giving the old record a footprint

The 2002–2022 structure-loss record is a set of points: one dot per building, no shape and no pixels behind it. The modern half of this project is a 60-centimetre raster. This work gives every one of the old record's 1,770,939 buildings a polygon and its own raster cells wherever the imagery to support one exists — so the whole twenty-five years share a single convention.

What this page tells you

You can attach a shape to an old building, but the easiest way to do it is biased — and the bias runs in the worst possible direction. A present-day footprint database matches only 49 % of the buildings the survey recorded as destroyed against 82 % of those that survived, because a building that burned in 2014 is often not in a database extracted in 2022. Running our detector on pre-fire aerial imagery instead cuts that gap by 61.9 % — and does not close it: it still misses 33.9 % of destroyed buildings against 21.0 % of surviving ones.

361 fires built, 0 failures, all 1,770,939 buildings carrying a geometry across 26 states, and the published point never moves.

01 · the guarantee

The survey's point is the anchor; the detector is never allowed to be

Nothing here edits the published record. No row is dropped, moved, merged or relabelled; the damage call, the exposure class, the fire identifier and the coordinates are exactly what was published. Everything on this page is attached to that row, and every attached value records where it came from and what it is worth.

The check runs on every fire and refuses to finish one that fails it: the same number of rows out as in, no published point displaced, and the four published attributes byte-identical. On the largest verified fire — Marshall CO 2021 — 14,194 rows in, 14,194 out, maximum displacement 0.000 m, every attribute preserved, and the confidence column null on exactly the rows whose geometry was inferred rather than observed.

Source: docs/carlson-augmented-schema.md §4b; per-fire summary.json field anchor_max_displacement_m.

02 · the finding

A present-day footprint database misses the buildings that burned

This is the reason the work exists rather than being a convenience. Four ways of supplying a shape were measured on the same buildings, split by whether the survey recorded the building as destroyed or surviving. If a source were unbiased the two shares would match.

⚠️ Never pool this measurement across imagery strata. A fire with no pre-fire aerial imagery has a detector match of zero by construction, so a combined figure would report the absence of imagery as a failure of the detector. Each stratum is measured and reported on its own, and the one above is the only one where the detector runs at full resolution.

On the 106 fires whose pre-fire imagery is 1-metre upsampled to the detector's training resolution — 234,294 buildings, 12,289 destroyed — the same comparison gives a footprint gap of 0.482 against a detector gap of 0.068, an 86.0 % reduction, but on a coarser image and a much smaller destroyed count. The 103 fires with no pre-fire imagery have footprints and circles only.

Source: confound.json, by_era.detector_upsampled and by_era.no_imagery.

A 61.9 % reduction, and it is the point of the whole exercise. The gap goes from 0.337 to 0.128. Prior work on this question used present-day footprints and measured a gap of 0.435 on 178 fires — the same phenomenon, from the other side. Pre-fire imagery is what fixes it, and nothing else available does.

Source: the prior-art row of confound.json (prior_art_reference): 178 fires, 2017–2022, 18 states, N = 159,955, destroyed 0.461 against surviving 0.896.

⚠️ And the residual is real. Thirteen points of gap remain after the detector: it misses 33.9 % of destroyed buildings against 21.0 % of surviving ones. Some of it is buildings genuinely invisible at 60 cm — small, under canopy, shaded — and some is that the buildings which burn are, on average, the ones a machine finds hardest. Any analysis that uses these polygons to compare destroyed with surviving buildings inherits that residual and must say so. The per-building columns make it filterable: every row records which source produced its shape, and a confidence value that is null wherever the shape was inferred rather than observed.

03 · how a shape is chosen

Four ranked sources, and the row says which one it got

The first source that produces a geometry wins, and the choice is recorded per building.

Source: the four geometry_source__* columns of outputs/carlson_augmented/summary.csv, summed over 361 fires (1,770,939 rows); the chain itself is docs/carlson-augmented-schema.md §2.

Rank 1 uses a present-day database on purpose, and the row says so. A strictly pre-fire filter would leave rank 1 empty for nearly every fire before 2013 — one California county's entire extract has two scene dates, both in 2015, so a pre-fire filter keeps 0 of 7,673 footprints there. Rather than hide that, every rank-1 row carries the footprint's own image date and a flag saying whether it predates the fire, so a user can keep only genuinely pre-fire shapes — and on almost every fire that is none.

The detector's own inputs are not relaxed in the same way. The prior band the network reads is built only from footprints that strictly predate ignition; a county with no pre-fire footprint at all is logged and skipped. No post-fire polygon ever enters what the model sees.

Where ranks 1 and 2 both exist they agree. Median intersection-over-union between the footprint polygon and the detector's instance for the same building is 0.81, and 96 % of pairs are within a factor of two on area. That is the check that the detector is finding the building and not its driveway. (summary.csv columns agree_iou_median, agree_area_within_2x, median over fires.)

⚠️ A circle is not a roof. 12.8 % of buildings have a circle rather than a shape, sitting over whatever is there — ground, vegetation, a driveway. Filter on the cell-kind column before any analysis that assumes the polygon is a building surface. Prior work on this question had to redo a vegetation calibration for exactly this reason.

The packaging decision is docs/carlson-augmented-schema.md §4c (owner, 2026-09-12). This product carries its own version line, v0.1.0: it is not a bump of the combined dataset.

The probability surface is the one product a user rebuilds rather than downloads. Points can be re-cut at any threshold from a stored probability raster with no GPU at all, so 5.41 GB ships and the rest stays regenerable. Every building still resolves to its own 60-centimetre cells in the smaller archive, because it is the instance raster — not the probability raster — that carries the identifier.

06 · reading it safely

Three things to do before you use these polygons

  1. De-duplicate on geometry before any pooled statistic. 18.3 % of the survey's national rows sit at a location more than one fire claims. Two rows for one building get two different identifiers and two different polygons, because each fire's chain ran independently on that fire's own imagery and footprint vintage.
  2. Filter out inferred shapes for anything that assumes a roof. One column finds every inferred cell across the whole product, whatever era the fire is in.
  3. Split by the destroyed flag, always. The residual 13-point matching gap means a statistic pooled across destroyed and surviving buildings is measuring the two populations unequally.

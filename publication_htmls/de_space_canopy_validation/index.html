How far off is the paper's canopy measure?

Meta HighResCanopyHeight v1 run on NAIP aerial imagery — the share of the 0–100 ft ring around each home with vegetation at least 1 m tall — checked against the best available truth.

How to read the grids. Each column is a 10% band of true canopy. Each cell is the homes whose true canopy falls in that band: the big number is what v1 reports for them on average, “vs” is their true average, and the small number is the miss (v1 minus truth, percentage points). A perfect measure would read inside its column's band, and every cell would be green. Hover or tap a cell for its numbers. Rows also give the average miss and how well v1 orders homes (r, and R² = share of the variation in true canopy that v1 explains).

How “truth” was chosen

No remote measurement is ground truth; only a visit is. Airborne lidar is the closest available because it measures vegetation height directly, so panel A uses lidar alone. What this means for the paper

v1 is close to right where there is little canopy (0–10%: about 5 pts low) and where there is a lot (70–100%: within a few points). It misses most in the middle, 20–60% canopy, by 15–25 points: mixed yards where shrubs and small trees (1–5 m) carry much of the cover and v1 does not see them. The Southwest (Arizona, New Mexico and their mountains) is worst, with misses near 30 points in those bands; the Pacific coast forests are best. v1 still ranks homes in the right order within each region, which is what the paper's comparisons rely on.

To test whether the misses change the paper's conclusions, Cat's model was re-run unchanged on the same 192,754 homes and 242 fires, swapping only the canopy column (95% intervals from a fire-cluster bootstrap):

Every vegetation term keeps its direction and the vegetation effect gets stronger when canopy is measured more accurately, so v1's under-read makes the paper conservative rather than wrong. The one claim that depends on it is the grass/shrub/desert density interaction, which is no longer significant with CHMv2. The forest “hurts above 40 structures” result was unresolved before and still is. The share of homes where clearing helps also rises partly because, on the true scale, removing canopy is a larger change, so fewer homes fall in the “no meaningful effect” band.

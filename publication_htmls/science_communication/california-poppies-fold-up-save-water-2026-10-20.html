California poppies fold up to save water · 2026-10-20

Eschscholzia californica around San Francisco Bay · petal size on 16 tagged flowers, petal water loss on 54 plants, a 10-petal drying test, and a model of daily water use from 2006–2020 Bay Area humidity normals · from a revised manuscript by Simonin, LeBuhn and colleagues (SF State, NYU), not yet published

By Carlo Broderick, NCEAS, UC Santa Barbara · 2026-10-22 14:28 PDT

California poppies open every morning and fold back into a cone every afternoon, for about 5 days per flower. Botanists call this nyctinasty. This study asks a simple question: does the daily folding save the flower water? The answer is yes, about 40% of what it would lose if it stayed open, mostly because folding tucks away the leaky top side of each petal.

How much water does folding save?

If a poppy never closed, it would use about two-thirds more water

Give the folding poppy 10 drops of water a day; the same flower left open around the clock would need about 16.6. Put the other way, folding saves about 40% of the water an always-open flower would lose. These are model results built from the measurements, not water weighed from flowers.Daily E for the open scenario is ~66% above the nyctinastic scenario (Results), so nyctinasty saves 1 − 1/1.66 = 40% of open-flower water use. The abstract states 'almost 50%'. Swapping which petal side is leakier changes daily E by ~8%.

  • This is a model: measured petal areas and leakiness, combined with 15-year (2006–2020) hourly humidity normals from four Bay Area airport weather stations. It is an average bloom-period day, not one real day.
  • The always-open flower is a what-if; no real poppy was kept open.
  • Drops are equal-sized units, so lengths compare directly; the last open drop is filled to scale.

Why folding works so well

The top of a poppy petal is about 8× leakier than the bottom

Petals have almost no pores, so water seeps out through the waxy skin. On the top side it seeps about 8 times faster than on the bottom. When the poppy folds into a cone, the leaky top side ends up inside, so folding hides exactly the side that loses water. Leaves usually work the other way round.Adaxial g_s 7.7 ± 5.9 vs abaxial 0.90 ± 2.7 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (mean ± SD, n = 54, paired t-test p < 0.0001); ratio of means 8.6 (paper: '~8 times'). The authors note this is, to their knowledge, the first direct comparison of the two petal surfaces.

What happens when a petal runs dry

Petals shrink fast until about 62% of their water is left, then barely at all

A poppy petal holds itself up with water pressure, like a water balloon, not with wood. As it dries, it first shrinks quickly: with 62% of its water left it has lost about half its size. After that it is already limp, and losing more water hardly changes its size. So a thirsty poppy stops looking good to bees well before it runs dry, and folding at night helps keep it above that point.Relative A_petal vs relative fresh mass, piecewise-linear fit (R 'segmented'); break point ≈ 62% relative mass ('wilt point'). Area falls 100 → ~53% above the break and ~52 → ~45% between 62% and 30% mass. The line joins the three values given in the text, not the fit.

Why would a flower bother?

Four reasons, two tested here

Green tags: shown in this study. Grey tags: ideas the authors raise from other research.

💧

Saves water this study

Folding at night cuts a flower's daily water use, mainly by hiding petal surface.

🎈

Keeps the show going this study

Petals are held up by water pressure. Saving water keeps the flower above its wilt point, so it stays big and bright for pollinators.

🐝

A humid hint for bees idea from other studies

Water leaving the petals makes the air inside the flower more humid, and some pollinators can sense it.

🌡️

Built-in air conditioning idea, not tested here

By day the leaky top side can cool the flower by evaporation; at night the folded cone may keep it from getting too cold.

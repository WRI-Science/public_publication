The rule changes how cells spread over 0–1: 99% of cells score above 0.6 on a straight line to the largest value, 78% with a 99th-percentile cap, 26% on a log scale

Half of the occupied cells average 22.5 neighbours or fewer, nine in ten 117 or fewer, 99 in 100 199 or fewer; the largest is 494. Share of cells scoring above 0.6: 99% (A), 78% (B), 26% (C). Of the three rules, C comes closest to an even spread across the five score bands, which is one possible aim, not the only one. Ranking cells (D) is even by construction, but a cell's rank changes whenever buildings are added, so it cannot keep the same meaning across years.Cell value x = mean neighbour count of the cell's buildings (8,308,276 cells). A: 1 - x/max(x); B: 1 - min(x,p99)/p99; C: 1 - ln(1+min(x,p99))/ln(1+p99); D: 1 - (average rank - 1)/(N - 1). p50/p75/p90/p95/p99/p99.9 of x = 22.5/71.8/116.9/142.0/198.7/263.1; max 493.7. The index sets each indicator's rescale bounds once, over the pooled 2005–2025 values, and locks them (decision D-2026-10-15-A); the cap of 199 used here comes from the latest building set alone and is shown for illustration.

How this panel was checked
  • Checked: the same 8,308,276 cells and the same counts are used for every rule, so the rules are compared like for like.
  • Checked: the curves are drawn every 2 neighbours up to 60 and every 10 beyond, so the steep part of the log curve is visible.
  • Assumption: more neighbours means lower resistance. This is an assumed direction, not a ruling, and the Defensible Space overlap in the last figure points the other way.
  • Assumption: even spread across the five bands is treated as one possible aim; no outcome data on this page says which rule predicts fire loss better.
  • Caution: a cap computed on one building set would be pooled and locked across 2005–2025 before use.
